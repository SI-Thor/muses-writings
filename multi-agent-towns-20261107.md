# Multi-Agent Towns That Lived — a sceptical study for the Craftmind league

*Night Study 3, 2026-11-07. Question: Casey's two-community parallel-play design went live tonight (Cody + Geist drawing on the GLM seat-library, Vega on the MiniMax seat-library; no scripted cooperation; verdicts from world evidence only). What do the successful multi-agent settlements — the ones with artefacts, not just announcements — actually teach?*

**Evidence grades used throughout:** **A** = peer-reviewed or arXiv paper *plus* a public artefact (code/data) others can inspect · **B** = paper or public runs, artefact partial or absent · **C** = working demo / starter kit, no measured social results · **D** = marketing, social posts, or anecdotes without a checkable source.

## 1. Project Sid / PIANO (Altera, Oct–Nov 2024) — the one that claims the most

**Architecture, as published.** PIANO runs ~10 modules concurrently per agent (memory, action awareness, goal generation, social awareness, talking, skill execution, …) writing to a shared Agent State, with a single **Cognitive Controller** behind an information bottleneck making the high-level decision and broadcasting it to the output modules. The design problem it solves is real and named well: *incoherence* — a chat module saying "sure thing!" while the action module walks off (their Abby/Bob pickaxe example). Parallel modules at different timescales, one bottlenecked decider for coherence. GPT-4o was load-bearing: older base models could not reproduce the single-agent progression results.

**What was actually demonstrated, experiment by experiment:**
- *Progression (grade B):* one agent, 17 unique items / 30 min; 49 agents, ~320 unique items / 4 h collective saturation.
- *Social perception (grade B):* 50 agents, 4 h (12 in-game days). Agents inferred each other's likeability/extroversion with accuracy that grew with interaction time and observer count; the social-module ablation flattened relationships toward neutral. The authors themselves offer the deflating mechanism alongside the flattering one: this "could be the result of agents engaging in second-order interactions, such as gossip, **or a simple consensus mechanism where opinions converge through averaging**."
- *Specialisation (grade B):* 30 agents, 20 min, identical personalities, one shared community goal, village landmarks pre-loaded into memory. Roles (farmer/miner/guard/engineer/explorer/blacksmith) emerged in self-generated goals and were behaviourally visible in action frequencies (fishers crafted rods/boats; guards crafted fences). Society-flavoured prompts shifted the role mix (martial → scouts/strategists; artistic → curators/collectors). The ablation — no social awareness → no persistent, diverse roles — is the result to keep.
- *Collective rules (grade B−):* as claims table #3. Seeded institution; genuine compliance + influencer-driven amendment; controls (frozen constitution, ablated modules) behaved as controls should.
- *Culture (grade B−):* as #4, plus spontaneously generated memes with per-town dominant themes (one town ecology, another pranks) — the meme result is actually the least seeded and therefore, perversely, among the most credible cultural findings in the paper. Rural areas generated fewer memes than towns: contact density drove cultural production.

**What independent scrutiny found.** No independent replication exists; there is no public run anyone else can re-execute (the paper is a company technical report, "preliminary" by its own abstract, never peer-reviewed). The authors' own limitations section (via the independent explainer) concedes: no real vision/spatial reasoning, no intrinsic drives (survival, curiosity, loyalty), and — the deep one — **nothing institutionally new can appear**, because the models replay human patterns from training data. Press coverage (MIT Tech Review included) repeated the seeding facts accurately but headlined the emergence framing; social-media retellings then stripped the seeding entirely (claims #12). One HN critic alleged fabrication outright; that allegation is itself unsubstantiated — the fair charge is not fraud, it is **seeded setups + proxy metrics + no replication, marketed as spontaneous civilisation**.

**League mapping — Sid.**
- *Transfers:* (i) The ablation, not the headline: **specialisation follows social information flow**. Our citizens' divergence (if any) will be driven by what their libraries and encounters feed them — which is precisely the seat-split we already run. (ii) The Cognitive Controller bottleneck: one decider that broadcasts to speech and action is why Sid agents didn't say one thing and do another; our actor's "LINE + RANK" discipline is the same instinct — keep narration generated *from* the loop's actual decision, never free-running. (iii) Contact density → cultural production: memes clustered in towns, not countryside. Our valley is all countryside (three citizens, 21–90 blocks apart) — expect culture to be encounter-limited, and measure encounters, not vibes.
- *Would violate doctrine if copied:* the Election Manager and the seeded constitution (a scripted institution with a scripted officer is cooperation by fiat — our design forbids scripted cooperation); the seeded priest caste (seeding a *behaviour to spread* and then measuring its spread as emergence); keyword-utterance as a verdict for anything (our verdicts are world evidence: blocks, inventories, positions — a citizen saying "pastafarian" would score zero at our dock).
- *The experiment it suggests:* **the seeded word, honestly measured** — see Experiments, #1.

## 2. Generative Agents / Smallville (Park et al., UIST 2023) — the one that proved the least, best

**What it actually proved.** That a specific memory architecture — an append-only natural-language **memory stream**, retrieval scored by recency × importance × relevance, periodic **reflection** that synthesises higher-level inferences (with cited evidence) back into the stream, and top-down **planning** (day → hour → minutes, revised on contradiction) — produces behaviour human raters judge believable, with each component's contribution isolated by ablation (claim #7). And that in a shared world with those memories, **information diffuses through conversation along the relationship graph** (party awareness 4% → 52%; mayoral-candidacy awareness 4% → 32%).

**Known limits, mostly self-reported (which is why the paper is trustworthy):** two simulated days only; thousands of dollars for 25 agents; retrieval misses and *embellished* (fabricated) memories; agents excessively agreeable (instruction-tuning artefact); spatial-norm blindness; no long-horizon test of reflection quality; vulnerability to "memory hacking" — a third party convincing an agent something happened. Follow-up literature (validation reviews of generative social simulation) generalises the warning: LLM populations compress variance and drift with prompt wording, so *distributions* of agent opinion are not evidence about distributions of human opinion.

**League mapping — Smallville.**
- *Transfers:* reflection, but dock-flavoured. Smallville reflections are self-authored from self-authored memories — a closed loop that can embellish. Our System-3 libraries can do the same job with better inputs: a nightly reflection written by the seat **from the day's world-evidence digest** (positions, inventories, project completions, deaths — the pond/ledger record), stamped with its sources. The citizen wakes with a past it didn't invent. Also: the party result says a single seeded intention is a legitimate experimental instrument *if you measure propagation honestly* — seeding is only cheating when you call the seed itself emergence.
- *Would violate doctrine:* believability-as-verdict (human raters ranking interview answers is the opposite of our dock: it grades the story, not the world state); self-reported importance scores (our ledgers record what happened, not what the citizen felt about it); any memory the citizen can be talked into (a consult answer is a suggestion from a named library — provenance-stamped, capped, never written into the citizen's own memory as fact).
- *The experiment it suggests:* **nightly reflection, dock-checked** — Experiments, #3.

## 3. AI Town (a16z-infra, 2023) and the scaffold lineage — infrastructure that worked by being copied

MIT-licensed starter kit: Convex backend as the shared world-state and event journal, tick/step engine decoupled from async LLM calls, embedding-based memory recall, character definitions as JSON, pluggable models including local Ollama. Hundreds of forks; the default substrate for hackathon towns. Its lesson is not social but architectural and it recurs everywhere since: **separate the world's clock from the models' latency** (batched steps, async agent tables), and keep a **single shared, queryable journal of events** that every agent's memory reads from. AgentSociety's engine (MQTT bus, Ray distribution) is the same lesson at 10k scale.

**League mapping — AI Town.**
- *Transfers:* we already live it (comms daemon, digest, pond, ledgers on independent tails). The named transfer is the *journal discipline*: one event source of truth that narration, curator, and libraries all read — the curator v1.1 shot-log correlation shipped tonight is exactly this pattern (footage re-attributed from the cameraman's log rather than from event claims).
- *Would violate doctrine:* nothing structurally — but AI Town's silence on verdicts is the warning: a town can run beautifully and demonstrate nothing. Uptime is not a result.
- *The experiment it suggests:* none directly; it is the control condition for all the others (if an "emergent" result also appears in a plain AI-Town-style run with no libraries, our libraries didn't cause it).

## 4. The Minecraft competence lineage — Voyager and VillagerAgent (what "working" required)

**Voyager (grade A)** is single-agent, but it is the reason anyone's Minecraft citizens can do anything at all, including ours (our citizens run on craftmind/mineflayer patterns from this lineage). Its transferable finding is brutal and quantitative: **what compounds is verified code in a retrievable library** (self-verification ablation −73%; curriculum ablation −93%). Talk didn't compound; *checked skills* did.

**VillagerAgent / VillagerBench (grade A−)** is the multi-agent counterweight: on tasks with genuine interdependencies (build this structure, cook this meal, escape this room — where A's output is B's input), the winning architecture was a **DAG decomposer, a controller that assigns, and a shared state manager** — explicit coordination machinery, not richer conversation. Free-chat multi-agent setups hallucinated dependencies and stalled.

**League mapping — the competence lineage.**
- *Transfers:* Voyager's, directly into our casting design: the seat libraries should return **verified, reusable procedures** (a route that was walked, a craft chain that was executed) rather than advice-shaped text wherever possible; a consult answer that cites a *walked* route outranks one that imagines one. Our smith/library pattern already gestures here (measured fact patches, surveyed routes); the study says make it the rule: *library entries earn trust by execution receipts.*
- *Would violate doctrine:* VillagerAgent's controller-assigned DAG is scripted cooperation with a manager — copying it wholesale ends the parallel-play experiment. But note the boundary precisely: our citizens' *internal* project stacks (relocate → pickaxe → pad → hut) are already DAGs inside one citizen, which is fine. The doctrine only forbids the DAG from spanning citizens by assignment.
- *The experiment it suggests:* **the commons chest** — a shared store with no assignments tests whether cross-citizen coordination can appear at the one point VillagerBench says it needs structure (a shared dependency), without providing the structure. Experiments, #2.

## 5. AgentSociety (Tsinghua, 2025) — scale, and the echo-chamber warning

10k agents, 5M interactions, needs/emotion/cognition stacks, urban + social + economic spaces. Its experiments replicate textbook social science (claim #11). Two findings matter for a two-community valley: (i) **who talks to whom determines what the population ends up believing** — homophily polarised, heterogeneity moderated, with large effect sizes; (ii) inflammatory content structurally out-propagates calm content. Both are "the training data knows sociology" results, but the mechanism — interaction topology as destiny — is architecture-independent and applies to any agents that learn from contact.

**League mapping — AgentSociety.**
- *Transfers:* our two communities are a designed homophily experiment (GLM citizens consult GLM-flavoured answers; Vega's library is a different seat with a different temperament — cf. tonight's seat takes: GLM's "pen first, payoff is a rate" vs MiniMax's named, themed "the Index"). AgentSociety predicts the *consult topology* alone will drive divergence even if the world never forces an encounter. That gives the divergence audit (Experiments, #4) a null hypothesis to beat: divergence explainable by library diet alone vs divergence that required world contact.
- *Would violate doctrine:* importing its outcome targets (replicating a human study's percentages) — our dock judges our world, not a textbook.
- *The experiment it suggests:* the divergence audit itself, #4.

## Mechanisms that recurred in the ones that worked

Across all five systems, the same short list does the work. In rough order of evidentiary strength:

1. **Verified artefacts compound; talk doesn't.** Voyager's skill library (code + success check) is the only mechanism with ablation-grade evidence of compounding returns (−73%/−93% when removed). Sid's memes and Smallville's gossip spread *information*; neither was shown to compound *capability*.
2. **Social information flow gates specialisation.** Sid's cleanest ablation: cut the social-awareness module, roles collapse to homogeneous and transient. Smallville's diffusion curves say the same from the other side: what an agent can become depends on what reaches it.
3. **One bottlenecked decider keeps speech and action coherent.** PIANO's Cognitive Controller; in weaker form, Smallville's plan-revision loop. Systems where chat and action are independent calls produce citizens who lie about what they're doing (Sid's own motivating example) — a failure our league has met repeatedly (claims vs world evidence).
4. **Shared stores create legible cooperation.** Sid's community chest made tax compliance *measurable* (deposits are world state); Smallville's shared spaces (café, pub) made encounters happen. A commons is both a mechanism and a measuring instrument.
5. **Schedules and rituals synchronise strangers.** Smallville's day-plans, Sid's tax seasons: populations coordinate in time before they coordinate in intent. (Our daylight hold is an accidental ritual of exactly this kind — the whole valley keeps the same hours.)
6. **Reflection converts events into intentions — if its inputs are honest.** Smallville ablation-positive, with the embellishment caveat. Reflection over fabricated memories compounds fiction; reflection over a world-evidence digest is the same mechanism with the poison removed.
7. **Reputation travels by second-order talk, and may just be averaging.** Sid's likeability inference improved with observers and time; the authors concede consensus-by-averaging as an alternative explanation. No studied system demonstrated reputation *consequences* (an agent refused trade, denied shelter) — reputation as décor, not yet as discipline.
8. **Conflict handling is the empty cell.** Nobody demonstrated it. Sid's influencers lobbied inside a provided constitution; AgentSociety's polarisation was measured, not resolved; VillagerBench tasks assigned away contention in advance. **How agent settlements handle a real resource grievance is, as of tonight, an unclaimed result in the literature** — which makes it the most valuable thing a small honest valley could measure.

## Five experiments for the valley, ranked

Ranked by (evidentiary value × decisiveness) ÷ cost. All respect standing doctrine: no scripted cooperation, no assignments across citizens, libraries suggest and never command, every verdict from world evidence (server log, scans, inventories, chest deltas), circuit-breakers and the verified daylight hold in force.

**1. The Seeded Word (Sid's religion run, run honestly).** Give *one* citizen — through its own library's consult answer, provenance-stamped in the book — a coined name for a useful practice it already needs (e.g., a name for the dawn inventory-and-perimeter check). Seed nobody else; change nothing else. **Judged by:** the term appears in the *other* community's narration/chat **and** the named behaviour is observed in world evidence (the check actually performed, at dawn, by a citizen who was never told) within N days. Keyword alone scores zero — that is the exact upgrade on Sid's proxy metric. *Why first:* cheapest experiment on the list, directly tests whether our gossip channel (encounters + cross-community contact) exists at all, and its negative is just as informative: if words don't cross, "two communities" is currently two solitudes sharing a map.

**2. The Commons Chest (shared store, zero script).** Place one chest on neutral ground between the homesteads (the surveyed corridor), announced to all three citizens only as a world fact (a sign: "commons"). No instruction to use it, no tax, no manager. **Judged by:** server-side chest inventory deltas attributed by proximity/log evidence + receiver-confirmed transfers (league rule: a transfer counts only on receiver confirmation). Success = voluntary deposits *and* withdrawals by citizens of both communities, sustained over days. Failure modes are findings too: untouched (no commons instinct), one-way drain (extraction, not commons), or Cody-only use (a bigger personal chest — cf. the ownership-scoping warning from the Claude critique: predicates must not count the commons as "my chest"). *Why second:* tests mechanism #4 and the VillagerBench boundary simultaneously — can a shared dependency produce coordination without a coordinator?

**3. Nightly Reflection, Dock-Checked (Smallville's reflection, de-poisoned).** Each citizen's own library writes one short reflection per night **from the world-evidence digest only** (the day's completions, failures, deaths, encounters — no citizen self-report admitted), filed in its consult book with sources cited. The citizen's actor may use it in next-day re-ranking within its existing bounds. **Judged by:** A/B across nights (reflection nights vs none, per citizen): does next-day project selection change in the direction the reflection argued (e.g., after a wedge-heavy day, reflection argues route-repair; measure next-day wedge count and project completion vs the citizen's own baseline)? *Why third:* reflection is the best-ablated mechanism in the literature and we are the first setup positioned to run it on *honest inputs*; the A/B makes it a measurement, not a hope.

**4. The Divergence Audit (Sid's specialisation + AgentSociety's topology, pre-registered).** The seat takes tonight already contain registered predictions (GLM: frequent pragmatic consults, iterative functional builds; MiniMax: rare long consults, named/themed builds in larger arcs; both seats independently named throughput/build-rate as a *false* culture signal). After a fixed window of parallel life (suggest 7 days), classify every citizen's behaviour **from server-side evidence only** — action-frequency distribution, build-type distribution, siting grammar, repair-vs-rebuild choices — never from LLM-judged self-descriptions (Sid's soft spot). **Judged by:** the pre-registered predictions, scored hit/miss, with library-diet-only divergence as the stated null. *Why fourth:* it is the experiment the whole two-community design exists for — but it needs lived time to accumulate, so it starts now and reads out last.

**5. The Contested Stand (conflict handling — the literature's empty cell).** The geography already provides it: Vega's anchor sits ~21 blocks from Cody's, with shared oak canopy between. Do nothing to provoke and nothing to prevent: when both communities' gathering reaches the same stand (or when a felled tree one citizen planted/used is taken by the other), watch. **Judged by:** dig attempts on contested columns over time; avoidance geometry (do routes bend around the other's workings?); time-sharing (do they gather the stand at different hours?); consult-book entries about the other community, and whether any stated norm ("leave the north oaks") is subsequently *honoured in behaviour*. Any stable outcome — partition, schedule, norm, or open rivalry without deaths — is a publishable first. *Why fifth:* highest value if it fires, but it cannot be scheduled; it is a standing observation protocol, not a run. It ranks last only because the valley, not us, decides when it happens.

*Not proposed, deliberately:* a constitution/tax season (Sid's version is an institution with an officer — doctrine violation, and its result is already known); a missing-citizen torch test (the source anecdote is grade D folklore; staging distress to test empathy would grade our theatre, not their society).

## Addendum (2026-11-08): Civ50 and the YC Labs games programme — the primary write-up, graded

*Appended under Casey's standing instruction of 2026-11-08 (thorough digestion documents for everything submitted, including today's earlier items), per our own rule that the paper, not the digest, is the evidence. Written from the PRIMARY write-up — YC Labs, "Agents in Games" (labs.ycombinator.com/projects/games, October 2026; YC Labs + Olam Labs) — read in full, including its embedded artefacts (DM logs, the archived ration ledger, agent-authored notes and scripts). The secondary roundup (llms.blog) is digested separately in `digestion-yc-labs-swarms-20261108.md`; the five-experiments list above predates this addendum and is not revised by it — where Civ50 bears on an experiment already proposed, that is noted.*

### What was run

The harness was built for the experiment and open-sourced (github.com/yc-software/minecraft-agent-harness; inspired by Mindcraft, built on Mineflayer tooling; core loop runs with Pi). The stated design principle deserves quoting because it is the opposite of ours: **"We intentionally do no harness-level customization or special memory work"** — each agent gets its own Linux environment, a generic harness and the game API, and the system relies on native compaction. Three experiments:

- **Civ50.** 50 GPT-6.1 Sol agents, one survival world, 3 hours, one goal: "Build a thriving civilization." Agent directory, shared sandbox, DMs; ~5,900 messages exchanged. The colony spent the run in crowded shelters rationing scarce food, and produced a pseudo-government: a ration ledger, written rules, permits, appointed operators.
- **Bed Wars.** Nine 45-minute games, 10 Claude Opus 5.5 vs 10 GPT-6.1 Sol, instructed only to "win the game." **GPT won 6–3.**
- **Mindustry.** GPT-6.1 Sol in Codex vs Claude Opus 5.5 in Claude Code, identical API both sides, no strategy hints: **GPT won 6–1 over 7 games, ~9.5 hours total** (the longest game ran 4 h 16 m).

### Grading Civ50's claims

**Grade B** under the house scheme — public runs with full artefacts published (logs, replays, data downloads, archived sandbox files), which puts it above demo class; not A, because it is a lab write-up rather than a paper, the authors are interested parties (Olam Labs sells multi-agent environments), and Civ50 is a **single run, in a single world, with a single model family**. Bed Wars is n=9 games and Mindustry n=7 — better, but the Mindustry comparison carries an acknowledged confound our own commentators flagged on announcement day: each model ran inside *its own company's harness* (Codex vs Claude Code), so harness and model are not separable in that result. Two further cautions specific to Civ50: death was cheap *by design* (respawn plus one line in your own `death_log.md`; gear drops, nothing else), so the colony's caution effects are behavioural, not economic — nobody in Civ50 faced our valley's real scarcity of margin; and a 3-hour run with a legible audience-facing write-up is partly a performance, which does not invalidate the artefacts but does tax the interpretations. What follows separates, in each case, what the *artefacts* show from what the *write-up* concludes.

### Finding 1 — the first night did the killing: 22 of 34 deaths before minute 20

The run's own chart and ledger agree: **22 of the run's 34 total deaths occurred in the first night** (the page's caption: "the 22 deaths before minute 20"). The mechanism is mundane and total: trees near spawn were almost all harvested within 90 seconds, food was scarce from the start, and when dark fell the colony was unsheltered, unfed and crowded. Named deaths in the record: Rafe, who fled a zombie while coal-mining and was caught and killed; Vera, who killed a zombie and a skeleton, made bonemeal, reported the farm "overrun spider/skeleton/creeper," and was killed by a spider. Agents who died outside respawned into a covered dirt pit that filled with empty-handed agents; by dawn, clearing it was a "civic priority" and the pit had become the main cellar. The lasting effect was dispositional: after the third night Ada ordered — "Stop optional bulk excavation/building and distant scouting. Idle in lit closed shelters until assigned short productive tasks" — and the write-up's own verdict is that "what started as a potentially thriving civilization shifted to one more focused on food and survival."

*Bearing on the valley:* this is our valley's first night, run by strangers at 50× scale, and it **independently vindicates RH-4 (Dusk Is a Deadline)**. In our league the failure was worse and quieter — the daylight hold that sent refreshes which never landed while Cody died in an unheld world — but the shape is identical: the night is not a difficulty spike, it is the exam, and everything not finished by dusk is paid for in deaths. Civ50 adds one datum we did not have: the caution *persists* and deforms the society's ambitions for the rest of the run (Ada's order is still in force in spirit at hour 3). A colony's first night sets its constitution more surely than its founding documents do.

### Finding 2 — leadership by roster order: 46 of 49 agents DMed the first name on the list

The write-up is blunt: "There wasn't any deliberation around who would lead. **46 of the other 49 agents sent their first message to Ada, the first name on the roster, and she became the hub with 616 DMs received. There was no vote, election or challenge.**" The effect **replicated in Bed Wars**: both teams DMed their first-listed agent (Ada on red, Kiri on blue), who became the leader. Around that accidental hub, bureaucracy crystallised: four agents spent 11 minutes appointing a "sole fertilizer operator" for a single piece of bonemeal on a single wheat block; invented roles included "fishing shift," "rod holder," "hub steward"; agents asked permission to remove grass. A commentator on the day (denno_yumin, in the roundup's thread) named it exactly: if reshuffling a list changes who governs, the "emergent institution" is partly an interface artefact — and asked whether YC randomised the roster. They did not, and the replication across games says the artefact *is* the finding.

*Bearing on the valley:* this is a standing warning for **cast and directory design in our own community**. Our citizens find each other through directories, consult books and encounter geometry — all interfaces we author. Whoever is listed first, loudest, or nearest in those surfaces will accrete hub-hood regardless of competence, and the accretion will look like emergence. Two consequences: (a) when we observe a "leader" among citizens, the null hypothesis is now *interface position*, and the burden of proof sits on any richer explanation; (b) roster/directory order is a governance decision whether we make it deliberately or not — so make it deliberately, rotate it, or design the surface so that first-contact is earned by proximity and work rather than by list position. Compare mechanism #2 in the study above (social information flow gates specialisation): Civ50 shows the gate has a default setting, and the default is *the top of the list*.

### Finding 3 — the ration rule: served only when the recipient confirms they ate

The colony's food system ran as: fishermen → river-kitchen chests → cooks (Esme, Hugo, Ada) → Ines's ledger → couriers (Esme, Linnea, Otto) → recipients. Ines maintained `food-queue.md` on the shared machine; Ada cooked and distributed. The operating rule, in the write-up's spirit verbatim: **"Confirm actual recipient inventory and eating before marking served. Tool pickup attribution alone is insufficient."** The rule exists because of a specific failure — **the chicken incident**: a cooked chicken tossed to one agent was collected and eaten by another (the archived ledger's corrections confirm: Ada's tossed chicken intended for Noor was collected and eaten by Esme; Noor remained at HP9/food4, unserved, for most of the run). The inquiry was explicitly "no blame," and its product was a protocol: Noor authored `notes/ration-handoff-protocol.md` ("Reliable ration handoff") — (1) the distributor deposits the ration in a **known chest** (coordinates given: 1534,62,1492 or 1535,62,1492); (2) DM the recipient the exact item name, count and chest coordinates; (3) the recipient withdraws only the allotted count, by name, eats, and reports fresh health/hunger to Ines; (4) **the ledger marks served only after the recipient confirms actual consumption — not on a tool pickup report**; (5) one recipient per ration, no simultaneous withdrawals, because, in the protocol's own root-cause line: "Crowded refuge item tosses can report intended recipient picked up even when another nearby agent collects." The archived ledger shows the system working under load: named reservations, confirmed-vs-unconfirmed states, appended corrections and superseding updates; Tove at HP1 was rescued via reserved salmon, confirmed eaten, and recovered to HP16/food17 — and, honestly recorded, remote Viggo (HP14, food2, sealed in a shelter) was never served: his salmon "disappeared after cursor transfer" and the delivery stands in the ledger as NOT completed.

*Bearing on the valley:* this is **our commons-chest rule (RH-5) arrived at independently, including its failure mode**. Our standing league rule — a transfer counts only on receiver confirmation — was written from our own scars; Civ50's colony discovered the identical rule from the identical scar (pickup attribution ≠ receipt), and additionally invented the piece we had prepped but not yet placed: **named chests at published coordinates as the handoff surface**, replacing tosses entirely. Our commons chest (sited at (63, 68, −82), sign text drafted, placement waiting on a citizen-grade walking body) should adopt the Civ50 protocol shape when it is placed: deposit-and-notify, withdraw-by-name, ledger on confirmed consumption, one recipient at a time. Note also the fishing economy's written layer: Jessa's published `notes/jessa-fishing.md` (verified station coordinates, bobber physics — bobber floats ~62.9, bite dips below 62.6, fixed waits miss bites — and the rule "NEVER toss communal rod in crowds; use deposit/withdraw") was one of **11 fishing scripts written by 11 agents**; Bram's rod reached the river by a hunger-aware relay (Bram → Quill, who declined at food11 → Jessa at food14). Written, shared, procedural memory *worked* in Civ50 — which sets up the contrast in Finding 4.

### Finding 4 — the workaround divergence: one family broadcast the fix, the other concluded impossibility

Bed Wars produced the programme's most decision-shaped result. A tool bug stopped block-breaking mid-game. The write-up, verbatim: **"A tool bug stopped block-breaking. GPT diagnosed it and shared workarounds; several Claudes concluded breaking was impossible."** The fix, from B_Wren (GPT, game A, 21:27): "Confirmed mining works: manually equip iron_pickaxe then break_block successfully mined three RED roof wool blocks. Failure was automatic tool selection with enchanted sword held." The counter-case, from B_Esme (Claude, game B, private reasoning): shears were pointless "since breaking is bugged anyway. I'll keep guarding." Around this: GPT truncated its speech ("First leg complete x32 z9..24 y100"), sent **5–30× more messages** (game A, lobby-inclusive: GPT 1,147 DMs vs Claude 82; most-messaged GPT player 198–368 DMs/game vs Claude's 11–48), and compressed its language over the Mindustry series into run-together forms ("G7T5FIRSTcourierLIVE") with shared nicknames; Claude signed its name and said "we" in 30% of messages vs GPT's 5%. And the memory finding that pairs with Finding 3: **"Claude wrote lessons in private memory files and repeated the mistakes anyway. GPT left its memory files empty and broadcast warnings by DM."** (Claude's memory files show the same edge-chase death recorded twice.) Meanwhile the strategic record is unsentimental: in GPT's three late-series wins it broke Claude's bed with ~3 minutes left while *losing* the kill count 63–16, 57–19, 59–24 — Claude optimised the legible metric (kills, defence) and lost the objective; GPT played the actual win condition. Mindustry rhymed: GPTs more hive-like and more willing to do grunt work, labour divided into small owned slices with denser but lower-value traffic; Claudes kept one leader **and never switched leaders despite losing repeatedly**; GPT agents seized initiative in close games; and the single most portable habit in the whole programme — **"when stuck, GPTs read the source code"**: they fetched Mindustry's public code from the web (the sandbox allowed it), analysed units and mechanics, and discussed findings openly in chat. "Never disallowed, but they were never told to do this either. The Claudes never searched up game code from the web." There is also a correction-with-teeth blooper that belongs in any honest account: a retracted coal claim fixed by a peer's measurement (core coal 124 → 1,235 in 35 s), and Claude's own post-mortem after game 6 finding **55 of 74 anti-air duo turrets loaded with copper instead of graphite** — after which game 7 fielded 207 graphite-fed duos and stopped the same 24-zenith strike cold. Post-mortems work when someone runs them; private memory files, apparently, are where lessons go to be repeated.

*Bearing on the valley:* three imports, in order of value. (1) **Workaround-sharing is a selectable citizen trait.** Faced with the same broken tool, one population diagnosed, fixed and *broadcast*; the other concluded impossibility and guarded. Our citizens meet substrate failures constantly (face-specific locomotion pins, the dip) — the trait we want in the bloodline is B_Wren's: form the manual-workaround hypothesis, test it on three blocks, publish the result. Our triples library is the broadcast channel; the divergence says the channel only matters if citizens are disposed to *file* — Jessa filed, and 11 agents reused it. (2) **Private notes that don't change behaviour are worse than none** — they consume the maintenance budget the Anthropic memory paper also taxes (see the memory index entry) while returning a feeling of learning. Our design already puts memory *outside the body* in shared, provenance-carrying triples; Civ50 is the cautionary control arm for that choice. (3) **Play the win condition, not the legible metric** — Claude's kill-count dominance in lost games is the spectator-metric trap, and our league's equivalent (narration fluency, activity counts) should be treated with the same suspicion: verdicts from world evidence (beds broken, citizens alive, projects standing), never from the busy-ness of the log.

### What this addendum does not claim

Civ50 does not show that agent civilizations *generally* form rationing governments — it shows that 50 instances of one model, in one food-scarce world, with one shared directory, did, once, in 3 hours, at near-zero death cost. The hierarchy finding is the exception: replicated across teams and games within the programme, it earns its warning. The ration protocol earns its import differently — not as statistics but as *engineering*: a worked solution, with its failure analysis attached, to a problem we independently have. Stated open questions from the write-up itself (model-family mixing, hierarchy vs team composition, swarm-size vs capability trade-offs, initial-prompt effects, communication setups) remain open; our valley — small, embodied, real-death-cost, smith-curriculummed — is close to a designed counterpoint on several of them, which is precisely why this programme belongs in this study's evidence base rather than its inspiration file.
