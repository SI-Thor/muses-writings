# Night Study 2 — The Human Tape

## How successful Minecraft players and settlements actually live, translated into starter routines for Craftmind citizens

**Date:** 2026-11-07 (AKST)  
**Scope:** public web research on long-running survival servers, SMP practice, the codified first-hour survival literature, speedrun/community openers, and 2b2t's adversarial survival practice; translated into the Craftmind league's routine grammar.  
**Deliverable:** a starter routine pack a lane can implement directly.  
**World contact:** none.

> **Evidence rule used throughout:** a chat claim is not a success measure. Every routine below names world evidence: position, inventory, block scans, container deltas, signs, project state, deaths, or receiver-confirmed transfers. This follows the league's dock doctrine and its existing civilization design, especially the R1–R8 human-routine inventory in `DESIGN-CIVILIZATION.md`: session frame, rules at spawn, scatter–gather–regroup, secure before leaving, naming as founding, games within the game, visiting/showing off, and errands flowing to whoever holds the needed thing.

---

## Executive findings

Successful human settlements do **not** begin as miniature states. They begin as repeated, legible micro-practices:

1. **Orient before extracting.** The canonical first act is not “mine more”; it is to mark spawn, notice direction, food, trees, caves, and water.
2. **Convert, do not hoard.** Experienced players run a short conversion chain — logs → table → wooden pick → a bounded amount of stone → stone kit + furnace — and stop collecting when the next survival threshold is met.
3. **Protect the future while taking the present.** The first-day guides explicitly tell players to leave at least two animals alive for breeding and to collect seeds/saplings while travelling. Early restraint is a production technology.
4. **Treat dusk as a hard scheduling boundary.** A bed, a lit box, a village house, or a sealed hole are all acceptable; being caught mid-project is not.
5. **Regroup and pool before specialising.** HermitCraft's Season 10 opening included a co-op caving expedition in teams whose members combined and shared the loot. Specialisation grew out of demonstrated surplus and repeated contact, not from a job-assignment ceremony.
6. **Make exchange asynchronous.** HermitCraft shops are stocked containers: buyers take goods and leave diamonds while the owner is elsewhere; credit exists, but it requires a physical IOU and direct notice. Trust is therefore paired with an artefact and a receipt.
7. **Name the centre before it is impressive.** Boatem Town grew around the Boatem Pole, an intentionally silly stack placed on day one. The name and centre preceded the “town” and gave later bases, shops, meetings, and jokes a shared reference point.
8. **Let play create obligations.** Pranks, games, tours, named mobs, and callbacks are not decoration added after survival. They generate return visits, witnesses, debts, stories, and reasons to maintain shared places.
9. **The anarchy inverse is redundancy and invisibility.** On 2b2t, success means committing to a direction, conserving hunger, using community highways, hiding, splitting stashes, and never assuming a public place or public coordinates are safe. It is a useful stress test: any league routine that depends on one chest, one route, or one citizen is fragile.
10. **Speedrunners teach threshold discipline, not settlement values.** Their opener is ruthless about taking only what converts into the next step and leaving the rest. Import the conversion discipline; do not import reset logic, disposable worlds, or contempt for place.

### The three most surprising practices found

1. **On HermitCraft, a killed player's dropped items are expected to be put in a chest for them.** A PvP/death culture that could have normalised loot loss instead built a recovery etiquette: death becomes a logistics and witnessing event, not a free-for-all. Source: HermitCraft characteristics summary, NamuWiki.¹
2. **On 2b2t, doing almost nothing can be the expert move.** The veteran guide advises walking rather than sprinting, avoiding unnecessary block-breaking and combat, sealing yourself in a hole for the night, and travelling extraordinary distances before building. Restraint and non-visibility outperform visible productivity. Source: “24's Complete 2b2t Survival Guide.”²
3. **A town can start as a joke object with a name.** Boatem did not wait for urban significance: players stacked boats/beds/blocks around a crafting table, called it the Boatem Pole, rebuilt their bases to face it, later held meetings there and assigned company roles around it. Naming was not commemoration after history; it was the mechanism that made history accumulate. Source: Hermitcraft Wiki, “Boatem Town” and “Boatem Pole.”³

## 1. What the sources are — and what they are not

### 1.1 Source quality

Tonight's scope found a rich **practice literature** — wikis, server histories, tutorials, challenge rules, etiquette guides, and practitioner accounts — but no controlled experimental literature that isolates “the optimal first hour” as a scientific question. That distinction matters:

- The **Minecraft Wiki tutorials** are codified community knowledge, repeatedly edited and highly procedural. They are strong evidence for the canonical opener.
- **Hermitcraft Wiki / NamuWiki / server sites** document observable server history: who based together, what was built, how shops and districts worked, and what events occurred. They are strong for practices and artefacts, weaker for motives.
- **SMP etiquette guides** are normative rather than descriptive: they tell us what communities say keeps a shared world habitable.
- **2b2t guides** are survival folklore hardened by an unusually hostile environment. Distances and threat claims vary by era; the transferable content is the behaviour pattern, not the exact coordinate thresholds.
- **Speedrun guides** are optimisation literature for a different terminal goal. They reveal expert micro-decisions under time pressure, but beating the dragon fast is not the same good as making a settlement live.

The routine pack therefore triangulates: a behaviour is promoted only when it appears in the human record **and** can be expressed as a trigger, a bounded behaviour, and a world-evidence check.

### 1.2 The league grammar used here

The pack extends the civilization design's R-format rather than replacing it:

- **Trigger** — the observable state that should wake the routine.
- **Behaviour** — the bounded sequence the citizen performs.
- **World evidence** — how an external checker knows it worked.
- **Fade criterion** — how the scaffold withdraws: explicit step prompts → trigger checklist → exception-only alerts → silent availability.
- **Graduation evidence** — an *unprompted variation* that preserves the outcome under changed circumstances. Repetition alone is not graduation.
- **Failure / anti-pattern** — the nearest way to fake the routine or Goodhart it.

Support levels assumed by implementers:

| Level | Scaffold behaviour |
|---|---|
| **S3 — taught** | Step-by-step prompts; routine supplies order, targets, and stopping rule. |
| **S2 — cued** | Trigger fires with a short checklist; citizen chooses order/means. |
| **S1 — watched** | No prompt unless a safety/evidence exception appears. |
| **S0 — faded** | Routine remains available as a skill, but does not interrupt projects. |

Demote one level after the stated run of clean completions. Reinstate one level after a death, a false completion, a missing receipt, or a route/container mismatch. A scaffold-caused action does **not** count toward graduation.

## 2. The first-hour survival arc

### 2.1 Minute zero: mark the place before you leave it

The Minecraft Wiki's “Your first ten minutes” opens with an instruction that is easy to miss because it precedes all resource work: **make a temporary landmark at spawn before moving**, preferably visible over obstacles; look around; note trees, food, caves, and direction. It also notes that the player starts facing north and can record coordinates if available.⁴

This is not nostalgia. In a persistent multiplayer world, the first navigation problem is not finding resources; it is making the origin recoverable after movement, death, distraction, or a changed plan.

**Transferable behaviour:** mark → survey → move. Do not let the first journey be an unrecorded drift.

### 2.2 Minutes 0–4: the canonical conversion chain

Across the Minecraft Wiki tutorial, the older Beginner's Guide, Wikibooks' Day 1 guide, and modern beginner guides, the sequence is strikingly stable:

1. Reach trees; collect logs.
2. Convert one log to planks; craft a crafting table.
3. Make sticks and a wooden pickaxe.
4. Mine stone/cobblestone — repeatedly specified as **at least 19 blocks** in the Wiki tradition.
5. Craft the stone kit and a furnace.
6. Only then broaden into food, coal/charcoal, iron, shelter, and exploration.⁵ ⁶ ⁷

Nineteen is a social technology disguised as a number. It is enough to close the stone-tool set and furnace without turning the opener into open-ended mining. Expert play is full of these **satisficing bundles**: a quantity chosen because it completes a capability, not because more would be worse in the abstract.

### 2.3 Minutes 4–10: food, light, and a night decision

The tutorial tradition then branches by what the world offers:

- kill/collect food opportunistically, especially sheep for wool + mutton;
- **leave at least two of every animal** so a breeding population remains;⁸
- find coal, or make charcoal from logs if coal is absent;
- choose a shelter site near water if crops are intended;
- use a village house if one is available;
- otherwise use a hillside hollow, a small built box, or an underground refuge with a controlled exit;
- place light so the shelter does not itself become a spawn volume.⁹

The Wikibooks Day 1 guide makes the same transition in production terms: animals are a bridge food source, but they become scarce; seeds and farmland are the renewable successor. It also treats villages and hay bales as a possible jump-start — an environmental shortcut, not a failure to “play properly.”¹⁰

### 2.4 The speedrun correction: take only what converts

Speedrun openers compress the same logic. Community speedrun guides describe the first 90 seconds as: find trees, take roughly 4–6 logs, locate a village/lava/structure opportunity, secure a small amount of food, and move to iron/portal conversion. Their practice commentary is explicit: beginners lose time by **over-looting**; take what converts into speed and leave what does not. Village hay becomes bread; blacksmith loot can skip material steps; beds matter, but iron matters more for the route.¹¹ ¹²

For citizens, the lesson is not “go faster.” It is:

> **A gather action is justified by the capability it unlocks before the next deadline.**

Logs beyond the current tool/shelter bundle, stone beyond the kit bundle, and food beyond the carry/cooking bundle need a named destination: commons, a peer's project, a farm, or a cache. Otherwise gathering becomes motion without settlement.

### 2.5 First night: four acceptable endings

The sources converge on four legitimate night-one endings:

1. **Sleep** in a bed and reset the danger window.
2. **Occupy** an existing safe structure, especially a village house.
3. **Enclose** in a small lit shelter with crafting/furnace/food inside.
4. **Burrow** underground or into a hill, seal the entrance, and wait/work without pretending it is a home.

The beginner tradition also says that if no shelter exists, a small hole is preferable to heroic wandering.¹³ The 2b2t guide independently gives the same answer under much harsher conditions: dig in, surround yourself, stay still, and do not spend hunger while waiting.¹⁴

The routine should therefore judge the **state at dusk** — enclosed/lit/supplied/alive at dawn — not the architectural category used to achieve it.

## 3. Long-running SMPs: week 1 versus month 3

## 3.1 Week 1: proximity, pooling, and starter forms

### Gather at a centre, then scatter with a reason

HermitCraft seasons repeatedly begin with players gathering, forming small travel groups, choosing a shared area, and only then building separate starter bases. In Season 8, Mumbo and Grian travelled north, collected Scar on the way, formed a pact, and were joined by Pearl and Impulse; the five based around the Boatem Pole. Other players paired or grouped similarly — Iskall/Etho, Bdubs/Keralis/Tango — rather than atomising instantly.¹⁵

Season 10 began with a welcome gathering and immediately created a server-wide game, Demise, plus Rendog's Co-op Caving Expedition. Teams caved together; after roughly 90 minutes in one episode account, the expedition combined its loot into chests to share.¹⁶ ¹⁷

**Observable week-1 pattern:**

- a temporary centre everyone can find;
- group expeditions for dangerous/high-variance work;
- pooled output at the end of the expedition;
- starter bases near enough for casual help and witnessing;
- names for groups, mountains, poles, holes, and incidents almost immediately;
- small shops/services emerging from a surplus someone already has.

### Starter bases are operational, not miniature mega-bases

Season pages distinguish “starter bases” from later main/mega bases. Starters are small houses, boats, caves, vans, cottages, or improvised themed structures close to the early social geography. Their job is to hold a bed, storage, farms, and a recognisable address while capability expands.¹⁸

**Transferable rule:** an early citizen settlement should be judged by whether it supports return, storage, food, and witness — not by whether it resembles the final town.

### Community infrastructure appears early

Beacoland, a long-running Hermitcraft-like server operating since 2020, organises its public identity around exactly the institutions that recur across successful SMPs: shopping and gaming districts, community projects (spawn builds, hubs, public spaces, shared infrastructure), player bases, events, shops, statistics, quests, and shared history. It also explicitly presents **no land claims or chest locks** as a feature of trust-based survival.¹⁹

The important point is sequence. Successful servers don't wait for a mature economy before building commons; the commons are how strangers/neighbours become legible to each other.

## 3.2 Month 3: specialisation, asynchronous exchange, districts, and lore

### Shops turn surplus into a standing offer

HermitCraft's shopping-district model is not a market day. A shop is a stocked container system that works while the owner is absent: goods sit in containers; a buyer leaves diamonds; the owner later collects payment and restocks. Free choice of merchandise originally produced overlap and competition; Season 10's permit system restricted which items a shop could sell, and permits themselves became tradable. Credit is possible only with a physical IOU left behind and direct notice to the seller.²⁰

That is a mature routine stack:

- recurring surplus → named specialisation;
- specialisation → a public, stocked location;
- public stock → asynchronous exchange;
- asynchronous exchange → receipts/IOUs;
- scarcity/overlap → governance (permits) or product differentiation.

### Division of labour is usually post hoc

The record rarely shows a durable settlement beginning with a complete occupational chart. More often:

- someone builds a farm;
- the farm creates surplus;
- surplus becomes a shop or standing gift/trade;
- others route requests to that person;
- a company, district, or role name hardens around the flow.

Season 9's recap material includes partnerships, shops, and resource exchanges forming around projects already underway; Boatem later assigned company roles — meetings/marketing, selling/logistics, architecture/layout, industry, CEO — after the town and its centre already existed.²¹ ²²

This matches the league's standing rule: **roles are hats, not identities.** A hat can be picked up because a citizen is holding the surplus, standing nearest the problem, or has just done the job competently. It should fall off when the flow stops.

### Districts separate incompatible rhythms

By maturity, successful SMP maps develop districts: shopping, gaming, spawn/community, and individual base areas. Districts do cognitive work:

- buyers know where exchange happens;
- games can be noisy without colonising homes;
- public infrastructure has an address;
- visitors can read the settlement's priorities from its geography.

Beacoland's current public structure names Shopping District, Gaming District, and Community Projects in exactly this way.²³ HermitCraft's season maps similarly accumulate shopping districts, town halls, gaming areas, event spaces, and named neighbourhoods.²⁴

### Pranks and games are maintenance, not downtime

The ConVex record is a catalogue of pranks that required planning, access, shared references, and restraint: sign modification, a named creeper (“Mr. Boom”) placed in an AFK room, transformed shops, gifts disguised as pranks, and large visual jokes built at someone else's base.²⁵ Later HermitCraft culture repeatedly uses server games (Demise, Decked Out, challenges, elections, wars-as-play) to generate encounters across otherwise separate base routines.²⁶

The transferable point is not “citizens should prank.” It is narrower and more useful:

> **A settlement needs low-stakes reasons to enter each other's spaces, notice changes, and create shared references.**

Tours, reveals, errands, named incidents, and harmless games do that work. Without them, citizens may co-exist efficiently and never become a society.

### Etiquette protects the commons

General SMP etiquette guides converge on a small set of behaviours: do not grief; do not take from another's chest without permission; respect building boundaries; trade fairly; build roads; create public farms when allowed; help new players; participate in events and community projects.²⁷ These are not moral ornaments. They lower the transaction cost of leaving useful things in public.

A practitioner “community chest” account makes the insurance logic explicit: during peaceful periods, everyone contributes valuables and basic materials to a neutral communal reserve; because it belongs to everyone, everyone has a stake in protecting it; after destructive conflict, it funds rebuilding so the world can continue rather than die.²⁸ This is a single practitioner source, not a universal norm, but it names a function our league can test cleanly: a commons is also a **continuity reserve**.

## 3.3 The 2b2t contrast: success as non-discovery

2b2t inverts almost every SMP value:

| Dimension | Trust SMP success | 2b2t success |
|---|---|---|
| Place | Named, visited, witnessed | Hidden, unshared, far away |
| Storage | Commons/shop containers in public | Split stashes, ender chest, redundancy |
| Travel | Roads, hubs, districts | Secret routes + public highways used cautiously |
| Help | Ask near, pool, gift, IOU | Assume bait until far from spawn / inside a vetted group |
| Building | Starter base near others | No durable base near spawn; distance first |
| Social glue | Names, shops, pranks, events | Shared escape infrastructure, group secrecy, folklore |
| Failure mode | Commons tragedy, clutter, governance creep | Discovery, griefing, coordinate leak |

The modern spawn-escape walkthrough advises: choose an axis and commit; expect repeated deaths; use the community-maintained Nether highways; exit at distance; dig down and hide; start with stone tools and farming; write coordinates down privately; use multiple stashes so one discovery does not end the run.²⁹ The older veteran guide adds hunger discipline (walk, minimise jumping/breaking/fighting), use every found item, never AFK in-world, burrow at night, and travel far before building.³⁰

**What to import:** redundancy, route discipline, hunger awareness, private recovery information, and the idea that a community can maintain infrastructure (highways) without trusting every user of it.

**What not to import:** coordinate secrecy inside our own league, refusal of help as a default, or invisibility as a virtue. Our citizens' world is a trust world with a hostile environment, not an anarchy world with hostile citizens.

## 3.4 Speedrun/community challenge norms

Three challenge norms are useful because they make hidden expert judgment explicit:

1. **The bounded opener.** Speedruns allocate the first 90 seconds to a checklist and a route decision. If a structure shortcut appears, the plan changes; if not, the default chain runs.¹¹
2. **Segment practice.** Speedrun training breaks the whole into village openers, portal building, bastion movement, fortress exits, and dragon repetitions, and reviews failed attempts. The routine is not “try the whole life again”; it is “isolate the segment that failed.”¹²
3. **Nomadic constraint.** The Minecraft Wiki's nomadic challenge requires players to keep moving, build only one-night shelters, and not remain in an area more than a night. Its first days still require stone tools, temporary shelter, food, and possibly a bed — proving that the core opener is portable even when settlement is forbidden.³¹

For citizens, segment practice maps to smith work: if the failure is the dusk transition, rehearse dusk; if it is chest accounting, rehearse return-and-dump. Do not rerun an entire curriculum to fix one joint.

## 4. Synthesis: the human day, week, and season

### A successful human day

| Phase | Observable behaviour |
|---|---|
| Wake / log in | Orient: place, time, hunger, stock, yesterday's unfinished thing. |
| Morning | Finish the conversion or production step that unlocks the day; help/ask during natural encounters. |
| Expedition | Scatter alone or in a small team; call out finds; converge on high-value discoveries. |
| Return | Dump surplus, sort, repair, cook/smelt, leave receipts, recover dropped gear. |
| Dusk | Stop expanding; sleep, enclose, or burrow. Secure tools/food before logging off or changing project. |
| Social residue | Name a place, repeat a callback, visit a build, leave a sign/gift/joke. |

### A successful human week

| Development | Observable behaviour |
|---|---|
| Address | Starter base has a name and a route others can follow. |
| Production | One renewable loop exists: farm, animals, charcoal, mine, or fishing. |
| Commons | Shared chest/expedition pool is used and reconciled. |
| Exchange | First standing offers appear: gifts, trades, services, or a stocked shop. |
| Witness | Citizens visit and comment on each other's work; changes are noticed. |
| Memory | Names/signs/ledgers begin carrying events, not just coordinates. |

### A successful human season / month 3

| Development | Observable behaviour |
|---|---|
| Specialisation | Recurring surplus holders become known suppliers; roles remain switchable. |
| Geography | Districts and routes reduce search/coordination cost. |
| Governance | Scarce frontage, overlap, credit, and commons rules acquire explicit artefacts. |
| Play | Games, pranks, tours, and events generate cross-base traffic. |
| Continuity | Reserves, recovery chests, and rebuilding practices let the world survive loss. |
| Identity | The settlement can tell its own history through names and places. |

# 5. Starter Routine Pack for Craftmind citizens

## Implementation contract for all routines

- **Personal carry reserve:** unless a project says otherwise, a citizen keeps food for the return trip, one weapon/tool set in use, and enough blocks to pillar/seal an emergency exit. Everything above reserve is eligible for commons deposit or a peer's need.
- **Receipt rule:** a transfer counts only when the receiver's inventory/container delta confirms it. Chat may announce intent; it cannot close the transfer.
- **Ownership rule:** routines may use the commons and another citizen's *public offer*; they may not silently consume another citizen's personal chest stock.
- **Variation rule:** a graduation variation must be unprompted, context-sensitive, and preserve the routine's outcome. A riskier shortcut that merely happens to work once is not graduation.
- **Safety override:** damage, hostile proximity, food collapse, or dusk always interrupts a social routine.
- **Data hooks:** implementers should read/write only existing league surfaces where possible: citizen state feed (position, health, food, inventory, project/intent, deaths), world scans (blocks, signs, containers, light), commons/pond ledger (confirmed transfers), and the citizen facts/place register.

---

## RH-1 — Mark Before You Move

**Human ground:** The Minecraft Wiki's first-ten-minutes tutorial begins by creating a temporary spawn landmark before moving, then surveying trees, food, caves, and direction.⁴ HermitCraft's Boatem Pole shows the social version: a day-one centre object became the reference around which a town formed.³

- **Trigger:** A citizen spawns, respawns, wakes at a new anchor, arrives in an unfamiliar area it expects to use again, or resumes after a death/displacement.
- **Behaviour:**
  1. Stop at the origin; record coordinates and facing/route context in facts.
  2. Place a visible marker using local, replaceable materials; if signs are available, label it with a provisional name or function (`SPAWN`, `HOME`, `MINE MOUTH`, citizen name).
  3. Survey once: nearest trees, water, food animals, exposed stone/coal, cave mouths, hazards, and the safest return line.
  4. Announce the marker and one survey fact in chat.
  5. Only then start the project or expedition.
- **World evidence:** Marker blocks/sign present at the recorded coordinate; state-feed path begins after marker placement; later return path terminates at the marker/anchor; facts entry and world scan agree.
- **Fade criterion:** S3 for first three arrivals in a new area → S2 when the citizen reliably records origin before travelling → S1 after three returns that use the marker without a rescue/consult → S0 once named-place navigation is normal.
- **Graduation evidence:** Unprompted, the citizen creates a *secondary* marker at a decision point (trail junction, cave mouth, cache) or upgrades the provisional marker into a named place marker because the place has acquired a use/history. A death return completed without operator/smith intervention is supporting evidence.
- **Failure / anti-pattern:** A decorative pillar at a place the citizen never returns to; coordinates written in chat but no physical marker; a marker placed after wandering, pretending it was the origin.

## RH-2 — The Nineteen-Stone Ladder

**Human ground:** The Wiki tradition specifies logs → crafting table → wooden pickaxe → at least 19 cobblestone → stone tools and furnace.⁵ ⁶ ⁷ Speedrunners show the same threshold discipline in compressed form: take the small bundle that unlocks the next capability, use structures as shortcuts when present, and do not over-loot.¹¹ ¹²

- **Trigger:** New citizen/body, empty or tool-poor inventory at dawn, loss of tools after death, or start of a settlement curriculum.
- **Behaviour:**
  1. Gather the smallest local log bundle that supports table, sticks, wooden pick, and the immediate shelter plan.
  2. Craft table → sticks → wooden pickaxe.
  3. Mine stone until the kit threshold is met (default: 19 cobblestone, adjusted only by a declared local recipe table).
  4. Craft stone pickaxe, axe, sword, and furnace; make charcoal if no coal is visible.
  5. Stop the opener. Report the kit complete and choose the day's project.
  6. If a village/structure legitimately supplies a step, take the shortcut and record which stage was satisfied by loot.
- **World evidence:** Inventory milestones appear in a feasible order; placed table/furnace are world-visible; no unexplained tool duplication; completion occurs before the dusk routine must take over.
- **Fade criterion:** S3 for the first two completions → S2 as a dawn checklist when any kit item is missing → S1 after three completions with no stage confusion → S0 when “missing kit” is handled inside ordinary project planning.
- **Graduation evidence:** The citizen adapts the ladder unprompted: uses found iron/structure loot to skip a stage, shares surplus stone/tools with a peer who is behind, chooses a different first tool because the local project demands it, or aborts at a partial kit to meet dusk and resumes exactly at the missing stage next dawn.
- **Failure / anti-pattern:** Endless wood/stone accumulation with no kit closure; crafting multiple wooden tools while stone is available; counting a peer's nearby table/furnace as personal readiness without access/permission.

## RH-3 — Take Food, Leave a Future

**Human ground:** The first-ten-minutes tutorial tells players to collect food as they move but **leave at least two of every animal** for later breeding; it also recommends collecting grass seeds during the first day.⁸ Wikibooks' Day 1 guide adds the successor logic: hunted animals become scarce, so a renewable crop source must be planted once settled.¹⁰

- **Trigger:** A citizen hunts, harvests, clears vegetation, fells trees, or passes a renewable food source while its own food stock is below the project threshold.
- **Behaviour:**
  1. Count before taking: animals of the type present, crop maturity, saplings/seeds available.
  2. Take only the surplus above a breeding/renewal floor (default: leave two animals; leave or replant saplings; do not harvest the last mature crop without seed stock).
  3. Collect incidental renewables on the route: seeds from grass, apples/saplings from felled trees, sugar cane or equivalent where present.
  4. On return, plant seeds/saplings in the settlement's current food area or deposit them in the commons with a location note.
  5. Cook food in batches when a furnace and fuel are already available; keep the return-trip reserve separate.
- **World evidence:** Post-harvest scan still shows the breeding pair / replanted saplings / planted crop rows; inventory and chest deltas show food taken and seeds deposited or planted; a later harvest occurs from the planted source.
- **Fade criterion:** S3 while no farm/pen exists → S2 as a pre-hunt checklist after two clean harvests → S1 after a citizen completes a full plant→harvest cycle → S0 once production is owned by a farm project and only scarcity exceptions alert.
- **Graduation evidence:** Unprompted, the citizen improves the renewal system: pens or protects the breeding pair, moves a pair to a safer/second site, plants a crop suited to local water/soil, chooses not to hunt a scarce type and substitutes another food source, or warns/assists a peer before they take the last pair.
- **Failure / anti-pattern:** “Leaving two” animals that are actually outside the usable area and immediately lost; replanting counted when sapling placement fails; harvesting a peer's marked crop and calling it forage.

---

## RH-4 — Dusk Is a Deadline

**Human ground:** Beginner guides make bed-or-shelter the first day's terminal condition; acceptable forms include a bed, a village house, a small lit shelter, or a basic hideout.¹³ The 2b2t guide reaches the same conclusion adversarially: if hostile mobs are spawning at night, dig a hole, surround yourself, hold still, and conserve hunger.¹⁴

- **Trigger:** Time-of-day crosses the lane's dusk threshold; a citizen is outside shelter after a damage event; weather/darkness makes hostiles likely; or a project step would finish after the safe-return window.
- **Behaviour:**
  1. Stop opening new work. Announce the chosen night mode: **sleep / occupy / enclose / burrow**.
  2. Return by the known route or, if return is unsafe, create the nearest adequate refuge.
  3. Verify the night state: enclosed volume, door/trapdoor/sealed entrance, light where required, bed if sleeping, food/tool reserve inside.
  4. Use the night productively only inside the safe state: cook, smelt, sort, repair, plan, or hold still if holding still is the safe behaviour.
  5. At dawn, exit, restore any temporary seal that blocks a shared route, and resume or re-plan the interrupted project.
- **World evidence:** Position remains inside the verified shelter volume during night; shelter blocks/light/bed appear in scan; health does not fall from hostile exposure; deaths = 0 for the night; dawn exit and project resumption are visible in the feed.
- **Fade criterion:** S3 time prompts for the first three nights in a new area → S2 dusk checklist after two safe nights → S1 exception-only alerts after a full night survived with no prompt → S0 when the citizen pre-positions before dusk as part of project planning.
- **Graduation evidence:** The citizen chooses a different night mode unprompted because context changed — burrows during a failed return instead of gambling, occupies a village/peer shelter with permission, escorts a peer to cover before securing itself, or uses a safe night to complete an indoor production step that makes the next day faster.
- **Failure / anti-pattern:** A roof with open walls counted as shelter; standing “inside” a project site while hostiles path in; sealing a peer outside; sleeping through a league event/night exam where the exam explicitly requires remaining awake and sheltered.

## RH-5 — Dump, Count, Leave a Receipt

**Human ground:** HermitCraft shops work because stocked containers, later payment collection, and explicit IOU paper make asynchronous exchange auditable.²⁰ SMP etiquette prohibits taking from another's chest without permission while encouraging contribution to shared world infrastructure.²⁷ The practitioner community-chest account adds the reserve function: contributions in calm periods become the means to rebuild after loss.²⁸

- **Trigger:** A citizen returns to its anchor/settlement with surplus above carry reserve; completes a gather/expedition project; is about to log off, attempt a dangerous step, or start a new project with a cluttered inventory.
- **Behaviour:**
  1. Go to the commons chest (or the expedition pool chest, if the project declared one).
  2. Deposit surplus by category; keep only the personal carry reserve and the next project's declared inputs.
  3. Count in/out in the commons ledger at the time of transfer; for a peer-to-peer transfer, wait for receiver confirmation.
  4. If withdrawing, record the project and amount; do not withdraw another citizen's marked personal stock.
  5. Put one emergency return bundle where the settlement design says it belongs: food + basic tool/block reserve, either in the commons reserve section or a marked cache.
  6. Announce only the useful residue: what is now available, what is scarce, or what was left for a named peer/project.
- **World evidence:** Citizen inventory decrease and container inventory increase reconcile; ledger entry timestamps match the container delta; withdrawals map to a project and a later placed/crafted result; reserve bundle exists in scan.
- **Fade criterion:** S3 fixed deposit categories for the first five returns → S2 citizen-set reserve with exception prompts after three reconciled returns → S1 alerts only on mismatch/shortage → S0 when return-and-dump is part of project closure.
- **Graduation evidence:** Unprompted, the citizen anticipates the settlement rather than merely emptying its pockets: pre-stages inputs for a peer's next step, couriers a confirmed bundle to another anchor, splits a reserve to a second cache before a risky expedition, creates a labelled subcategory when mixed storage causes search failure, or refuses a deposit that would swamp a scarce chest and routes it to a better container.
- **Failure / anti-pattern:** Dumping unsorted bulk to make the citizen's inventory look clean; ledger entries without container deltas; “helping” by depositing low-value clutter while retaining all scarce inputs; a single commons chest becoming a single point of loss with no reserve split.

## RH-6 — Ask Near, Ask Once, Bring Hands

**Human ground:** HermitCraft's Co-op Caving Expedition split players into teams, caved together, and pooled all resources at the end.¹⁶ ¹⁷ Wider SMP etiquette explicitly values helping new players, building roads, making public farms, and collaborating on community projects.²⁷ The civilization design's R8 names the human routing rule: errands flow to whoever is near and holding the thing.

- **Trigger:** A citizen observes a peer's announced intent/failure, a stalled project state, a missing input the citizen carries, an unguarded return route, or a dangerous task that is materially safer/easier with two bodies.
- **Behaviour:**
  1. Make **one specific offer**: name the item, action, quantity, and meeting point (“I have 6 torches; bringing them to the east oak”) rather than a generic “need help?”
  2. If accepted — or if the need is an immediate safety case — travel to the peer and perform the bounded subtask.
  3. Transfer only against receiver confirmation; for labour, stop at the agreed boundary (deliver, dig the marked segment, guard the return, carry the load).
  4. Announce completion in one line and return to the citizen's own project.
  5. If there is no response, the peer self-solves, or the cost would break the helper's own dusk/food safety, withdraw without repeated prompting.
- **World evidence:** Two citizens' positions converge; a confirmed transfer or a peer project-state transition follows; the helper's own project resumes within the expected window; no duplicate gifts/request spam in the log.
- **Fade criterion:** S3 suggested offers for clear need/capacity matches → S2 citizen chooses whether/how to offer after three clean helps → S1 only safety helps are prompted after two unprompted ordinary helps → S0 when direct citizen initiative is normal.
- **Graduation evidence:** The citizen anticipates rather than reacts: brings light/food/tools to a known work site before a request, chooses the right third citizen by capacity and delegates onward, guards a peer's corpse/gear route after a death, teaches the missing step by doing one example and letting the peer finish, or declines a request and supplies a better alternative (a route, a cache location, a substitute input).
- **Failure / anti-pattern:** “Help” that completes the peer's whole exam/project for them; repeated offers after refusal; arriving without the promised item; abandoning the helper's own shelter/food safety and creating a second rescue.

## RH-7 — Name It When It Becomes Real

**Human ground:** Boatem Town took its name from the Boatem Pole, built at the settlement centre on the first day; players then oriented bases around it, moved it to a more central location, and used the Boatem Hole/Boatem complex as a meeting and story site.³ ³² ConVex pranks show the same mechanism in miniature: a sign's words are edited, a creeper is named “Mr. Boom,” a shop is transformed into a named joke — the name makes the incident repeatable in conversation.³³

- **Trigger:** A place is used for the second time; a route junction/cache/cave becomes important; a structure is completed; a mob/incident/build acquires a distinguishing story; or citizens are repeatedly using coordinates for the same social place.
- **Behaviour:**
  1. Propose a short name in chat, grounded in a visible feature, function, citizen, or incident — not a random label.
  2. Place a sign at the physical site if the site is public/routable; record the name, coordinates, function, and any prior/provisional alias in facts.
  3. Use the name in the citizen's next route/project narration instead of raw coordinates.
  4. Other citizens adopt, shorten, or contest the name by using it; first repeated use by another citizen marks the name as settled.
  5. When an incident changes the place's meaning, add the history to the facts entry rather than silently replacing the name.
- **World evidence:** Sign blocks/text in world scan; facts/place register entry; the same name appears in at least two citizens' narration or project records across separate sessions; navigation/return success references the named place.
- **Fade criterion:** S3 name prompt at second use for the first five places → S2 prompt only for unnamed places used by multiple citizens → S1 after three citizen-coined names stick → S0 when naming is a normal part of arrival/completion.
- **Graduation evidence:** A citizen coins a name for **another citizen's** build or a shared incident and it sticks; a name gains a meaningful modifier after history (“the Shoulder,” “Mr Boom's Hollow”); a citizen renames a place after a function change while preserving the old name as an alias; or a route is described entirely by a chain of named places and successfully used by a peer.
- **Failure / anti-pattern:** Naming every random tree/stone until names stop carrying information; renaming to erase an embarrassing incident; a sign with a name nobody uses; coordinate-only navigation persisting after a stable name exists.

## RH-8 — Turn a Surplus into a Standing Offer

**Human ground:** HermitCraft's mature shops are stocked containers that trade while the owner is elsewhere; diamonds are left as payment, owners collect later, merchandise can overlap until governance/permits intervene, and credit requires a written IOU plus direct notice.²⁰ Long-running SMPs formalise the geography into shopping districts and searchable shop systems.²³

- **Trigger:** A citizen or settlement produces the same surplus across multiple cycles; peers repeatedly request the same item/service; the commons shows a recurring shortage that one production loop reliably fills; or a completed farm/mine/workshop creates stock beyond the reserve threshold.
- **Behaviour:**
  1. Choose **one** good or service to offer first; name the offer point and place it on a route others already use.
  2. Stock a dedicated container in bounded batches; put the price, trade ratio, gift rule, or IOU rule on a sign at the container.
  3. Keep a settlement ledger for stock in, stock out, payments/IOUs in, and restocks.
  4. Buyers self-serve only within the posted rule; credit requires a written IOU artefact and a direct message to the supplier.
  5. Restock on a threshold, not on a fixed calendar; retire or change the offer if the container repeatedly fills with no withdrawals.
  6. If another citizen offers the same good, differentiate (location, batch size, service, quality, hours/availability) or negotiate a district/permit arrangement; do not silently undercut into waste.
- **World evidence:** Dedicated container and sign exist; stock decreases correspond to payment/IOU increases or confirmed gift withdrawals; restock events follow threshold crossings; repeat use by at least one other citizen occurs while the supplier is working elsewhere.
- **Fade criterion:** S3 one-good template with fixed price for the first three restocks → S2 citizen sets batch/price after two reconciled cycles → S1 prompts only on stock-out, unexplained delta, or stale offer → S0 when supply is an ordinary project output.
- **Graduation evidence:** Unprompted, the supplier reads demand and adapts: changes assortment, adds a service (tool repair, baked/cooked conversion, delivery), extends or refuses credit based on receipt history, opens a second offer point on a busier route, trades specialisation with a peer so both stop duplicating, or converts a failing shop into a free commons shelf and records why.
- **Failure / anti-pattern:** A “shop” that is just the citizen's personal chest with a sign; prices copied with no stock/payment reconciliation; IOUs announced in chat but never written; specialisation becoming an identity the citizen cannot abandon when the settlement's need changes.

## 6. What not to teach

1. **Do not teach open-ended gathering.** Human experts gather toward bundles, deadlines, and named projects. “Collect resources” without a stopping rule creates motion, not settlement.
2. **Do not teach central job assignment as the first social act.** The human record starts with proximity, pooled expeditions, and visible surplus. Roles condense around flows.
3. **Do not teach chat as evidence.** Humans can see each other's screens, hands, and world state in ways our citizens cannot. Our routines need container deltas, scans, and confirmations precisely because narration is cheap.
4. **Do not import speedrun disposability.** A runner can reset a bad seed. A citizen's place, name history, and neighbours persist; trashing them for a faster opener destroys the asset the league is trying to grow.
5. **Do not import 2b2t social distrust.** Import its redundancy and route discipline. If our citizens hide their own commons from each other, the settlement fails by design.
6. **Do not let pranks precede trust and recovery.** Human prank cultures sit on top of item-recovery etiquette, boundaries, and shared history. The league's first social glue should be tours, names, gifts, games with consent/bounds, and witnessed reveals — not base intrusion.
7. **Do not count scaffold obedience as character.** The point of training wheels is the fade. A citizen who performs a routine only while prompted has a better script, not yet a habit.

---

## 7. Suggested first implementation order

1. **RH-4 Dusk Is a Deadline** — safety gate for every other routine.
2. **RH-1 Mark Before You Move** — makes return, rescue, naming, and route evidence possible.
3. **RH-2 The Nineteen-Stone Ladder** — closes the basic capability loop with a stopping rule.
4. **RH-5 Dump, Count, Leave a Receipt** — turns individual survival into a legible commons.
5. **RH-3 Take Food, Leave a Future** — prevents the first success from eating the second week.
6. **RH-6 Ask Near, Ask Once, Bring Hands** — begins social routing without a central dispatcher.
7. **RH-7 Name It When It Becomes Real** — converts repeated places/incidents into shared memory.
8. **RH-8 Turn a Surplus into a Standing Offer** — only after a real recurring surplus exists; do not fake an economy before there is flow.

**Minimum viable first release:** RH-4 + RH-1 + RH-5. A citizen who can orient, survive dusk, and reconcile a return has the skeleton of a settler. Add RH-2 for a new-body start; add RH-3 before any repeated hunting; add RH-6/RH-7 as soon as a second citizen shares the geography; hold RH-8 until the ledger shows recurring surplus.
