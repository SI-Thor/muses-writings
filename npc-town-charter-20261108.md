# The NPC Town — Charter and Specification

*Charter for the programme's new centre of gravity: a functioning NPC town in
generated downtown Sitka, run as a going concern, whose operating data compiles
down into reflexes and a theory of mind until the status quo costs a fraction of
what it cost on day one. Commissioned by Casey, 2026-11-08 17:37 AKST, in the
order of operations stated in §0.1. Design authorities: the Syndicate Protocol
study and its Addenda 2/3 (the four-tier stack, the Sitka board, the production
shape), the Syndicate-of-Four Pilot A spec (governor identity discipline, the
trigger-surface legality rule, receiver-confirmation scoring), the Sitka twin
fidelity loop study (the board's fidelity governance), and DESIGN-FRAME-TO-WEIGHTS
(the graduation pipeline, the state-independence boundary, the scratch-pad
doctrine). Substrate authorities: the league's OPERATIONS.md record and the
standing doctrines cited in-line. Where a number in this charter is a proposal
rather than a measurement, it is labelled PROPOSAL; where it is inherited from a
measured record, the record is named.*

---

## 0. What this document is, and how it was made

This charter was written by reading, in full and in this order: the Syndicate
study including Addendum 2 (paint + the four tiers) and Addendum 3 (the
production shape); the twin-fidelity study; the Pilot A spec including its
decode-settlement and survey-adoptions annotations; and DESIGN-FRAME-TO-WEIGHTS
end to end, including its adversarial §2.5 (the silently-uncompilable class) and
§3.4 (the independence problem). The writing decisions that shaped the charter,
stated here because the process is part of the record:

1. **The town is not the game with the paint removed.** The Syndicate design's
   centre is the round: script phase, drop, horn, census. A town has no horn.
   The charter therefore takes the study's *substrate* (the board, the tiers,
   the governor discipline, the failure-mode table) and replaces its *clock*:
   the round cycle becomes the town day, and Tier L's per-round reflection
   becomes the town's nightly crafting shift. Everything in the study that was
   round-shaped is re-derived against the day shape rather than copied.
2. **Graduation at town scale compiles to scripts and stores first, weights
   second — and the charter says why, in the design's own terms.**
   DESIGN-FRAME-TO-WEIGHTS governs compilation into weight modifiers, and its
   adoption gate is Casey's. But the town's behaviours run on bodies whose
   "frame" is the fleet's script-and-model stack, and the design itself names
   the text-tier analogue as prior art already operated by this programme:
   Voyager's skill crystallisation and the league's commons pond, where verified
   behaviour compiles into a library the reflexes retrieve from. The town's
   graduation pipeline (§3) is therefore two-rung: frame behaviour compiles
   first into **filed reflex scripts and structured stores** (routes, tables,
   the theory-of-mind ledger), which is where the compute saving actually
   lands; compilation further down into weights follows the design's pipeline
   unchanged, behind its gates, when a behaviour's dossier justifies it. The
   boundary the design draws — what may graduate at all — binds both rungs
   identically.
3. **Nothing in this charter launches a citizen.** The town's cast is new,
   genuine identities on the town's own server (§1.1, §7). Cody, Vega and
   Geist are not cast, not moved, and not consulted: they are parked citizens
   with their own seasons, and Cody's state is critical. The league's relaunch
   questions stay exactly where the league left them.

### 0.1 Casey's order of operations (2026-11-08 17:37 AKST), restated as programme law

1. **First: the town.** A little functioning NPC city in generated downtown
   Sitka — citizens with homes, rounds, errands and work, a status quo in
   operation. Procedural generation proceeds from there.
2. **The paintball game is written up completely and kept ready** — study,
   addenda, Pilot A spec, paint spike, survey adoptions, all filed — **but it
   is not the main effort until the town functions.** §6 states what the game
   inherits when it arrives, and what "arrives" means.
3. **The graduation economics govern the town from day one.** The town starts
   expensive on purpose: LLMs up high continually crafting and refining
   scripts, little decision models called per domain, the full frame in
   attendance. The simulation commences on that basis immediately — it does
   not wait for the cheap version. Every operating day's data is structured as
   it is produced, so that behaviour which proves itself compiles down into
   reflexes and the town's theory of mind, and the status quo takes less and
   less compute over time. §3 makes that sentence measurable; a charter that
   merely asserted it would be the claimed-but-not-done failure in a new
   costume.

## 1. The minimum functioning town

### 1.1 The cast: eight citizens, four trades, two of each

**PROPOSAL, sized from the record:** the minimum town that reads as alive is
**eight citizens**. The reasoning, not just the number:

- The league's three citizens, run one at a time, read as *subjects under
  study* — the episode-video verdict (Casey, 2026-11-08) was that isolated
  citizens standing around is the failure, and the character-first doctrine's
  remedy is citizens visibly *doing things among other citizens*. Aliveness is
  a property of the interactions visible in a single frame: a street with two
  people passing, a shop with a keeper and a customer, a worksite with a pair
  working. Eight citizens across four workplaces gives every public place a
  plausible pair and every errand a plausible counterparty.
- Four is the Pilot A cast size and is too few for a town: one syndicate reads
  as a team with a mission, which is the game's shape, not a town's. A town
  needs people whose business is *not* with each other most of the time — the
  background crossings are what make the foreground encounters read as life.
- Sixteen is the study's upper serving case and is not needed to prove the
  town; it doubles the expensive phase (§2.3) before the graduation curve has
  produced its first evidence. The charter's growth path (§1.5) adds citizens
  only behind measured headroom.

**The cast structure: four trades, two citizens per trade.** Pairs matter:
each trade has a senior and a junior, which gives the town its simplest
legible social fact — one who knows the round and one who is learning it —
and gives the theory-of-mind layer (§4) its first non-trivial content (the
junior models the senior; the town models both). Names are paint-free and
place-true in spirit — harbour-town names, filed at TOWN-0 with the cast
manifest; the charter fixes the trades, not the names:

| Trade | Workplace | The visible work | Why it earns its place |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Harbour hands ×2** | The harbour / Crescent Harbor shore | Hauling between the dock store and the boats; net-and-crate stock moving; the morning launch round | The waterfront is Sitka's front yard and the twin's most recognisable stratum; harbour work is *locomotive* — it reads at a distance, from the cameraman's and Casey's vantage, as purpose |
| **Shopkeepers ×2** | The commons store on Lincoln Street | Stock-keeping: shelf counts, restock errands to the dock store, the till ledger; opening and closing the shop | Stock is the town's first graduation candidate (§3.2) and its first receiver-confirmed economy: every transfer is a Noor-shaped event with a counterparty |
| **Builders ×2** | The worksite (first build: the town's own second row of houses) | Surveyed build work from filed plans; material hauls from the store; scaffold-and-frame progress visible day over day | A town that is visibly *being built* is procedural generation with the process left in — Casey's direction ("procedurally generate from there") made literal and watchable |
| **Keeper of the square ×1 + the carter ×1** | Cathedral square / the whole street network | The keeper: square upkeep, the notice board, the morning muster. The carter: the town's rounds made flesh — a continuous delivery circuit among all workplaces | The carter is the town's circulatory system and its best liveliness instrument: a citizen whose whole job is crossing everyone else's path. The keeper owns the town's one gathering ritual |

Citizens are **genuine fresh identities on the town's own server** — the
Pilot A spec's construction, adopted whole: a scratch Paper server (fleet
bundle, Java 25) whose `world/players/data/` starts empty, join-in-order,
`.dat` sha256s filed in the town manifest, presence re-verified in the LIVE
directory immediately before every launch window. The live league world is
never the town's substrate: its sample-clone behaviour makes genuine
identities impossible there, and its citizens are not the town's cast.

### 1.2 Geography: the district as a set of rounds

The board is the Sitka-gen district — Spike 1's output (the Lincoln/downtown
core crop of the study §5 pipeline: SRTM shape, charts coast/water, OSM pads
and roads, terraced per the triple-020 generation rule, sea level y 63) — and
the town's geography is defined **functionally, as the rounds that run on
it**, not as a building inventory:

- **Homes row** — the Lincoln Street house family (the Foundry `sitka_house`
  draws, re-emitted in blocks per the twin study): eight homes, one per
  citizen, each with a door, a bed, a chest. A home is the citizen's anchor:
  the day starts and ends there, and the theory-of-mind layer's first beliefs
  are about who lives where.
- **The store** — one commons store on Lincoln Street, stocked from the dock
  store, run on the Noor protocol's shape (named stock, deposit/withdraw by
  name, a transfer counts only on receiver confirmation — the league's
  standing rule, inherited verbatim).
- **The dock store + harbour workface** — the town's intake: goods arrive
  here (spawned by the town's supply rule, §1.3, receipted as deliveries),
  harbour hands move them up the hill.
- **The worksite** — one active build site at a time, at the district's edge:
  generation proceeding from the town outward, per Casey's direction. The
  builders' filed plan *is* the next piece of the town.
- **Cathedral square** — the gathering place: morning muster, the notice
  board (a physical sign wall the keeper maintains; the town's public
  scratch paper), the twin study's icon landmark presiding.
- **The street network** — the OSM roads, graded and surveyed: the only
  routes scripts may rely on are surveyed routes (league law), and the
  carter's circuit is the survey made continuous — every circuit re-verifies
  the network it walks, and a forcedMove anywhere on it is a town incident,
  filed with `fault_owner`, never a citizen's shame.

Distances are the district's own: at 1:1 the core is ~2.4 × 2.0 km, but the
*town* — homes, store, square, dock, worksite — occupies the walkable heart
of it, minutes end to end. Errands are sized so a round trip sits inside one
day-part (§1.3) with margin; a round that cannot complete in its day-part is
a charter defect, re-planned at Tier L, not a citizen failure.

### 1.3 The day rhythm, with the daylight lessons applied as law

A **town day** is one full cycle of the town clock: **dawn muster → work
parts → dusk muster → night**. PROPOSAL: the town day runs ~50 real minutes
(day parts under a held, verified daylight state; the night part short and
sheltered — see below). The rhythm:

1. **Dawn muster (square).** All citizens walk their home→square route; the
   keeper reads the notice board (yesterday's filings, today's deliveries,
   the worksite plan); pairs form. This is the town's daily *legibility
   event*: in five minutes a watcher sees every citizen, moving, with a
   destination.
2. **Work parts ×3 (morning / midday / afternoon).** Trades run their rounds:
   harbour circuit, shop hours with restock errands, build shifts, the
   carter's circuit continuously underneath all of it. Errands fire from
   stock and schedule state — a shelf empties, a delivery lands, a build
   stage completes — not from a script's clock alone.
3. **Dusk muster (square).** Returns, the day's confirmations read into the
   record (what moved, who confirmed, what failed), citizens walk home.
4. **Night.** Citizens are home and *sheltered by construction* (§7): the
   town's server regime keeps the night non-lethal (difficulty and spawn
   rules set so the night is a pause, not a threat — the town is not a
   survival exam; the league's citizens already sit that exam elsewhere).
   Tier L's crafting shift (§2.2) runs in this window, off the citizens'
   clock entirely.

**The daylight-hold lessons, applied without negotiation** (the hold-loss
incident is the league's founding wound: refreshes sent from ~05:58Z never
landed, the world ran real nights ~90 minutes, Cody's deaths went 38 → 114):

- Every daylight refresh for the town's day parts is **verified as landed in
  the server log** — the refreshed state read back from a store the refresher
  cannot write (Watcher's Charter Rule 1). A sent refresh is an echo, never
  a landing.
- **Two consecutive misses = HOLD-LOST**: the town clock alarms and parks —
  citizens' drivers stop issuing work, citizens hold position or shelter per
  their standing orders — until a landing is verified again. The guarded
  cameraman chain's discipline (201 verified landings at last record) is the
  reference implementation and its standard.
- The town treats itself as **unheld until a landing-verified hold is
  proven** on its own server, in its own log, at TOWN-0 — the league's rule
  carried over as a launch gate, not a hope.
- Time-of-day and gamerules are **read from the running server**, never only
  from files (the replication lane's divergence finding: the live league
  world's saved difficulty is NORMAL in `level.dat`, overriding
  `server.properties`' peaceful — the town's non-lethal regime is verified
  against the running server's actual state at TOWN-0 and re-verified at
  every Hold audit).

### 1.4 What the spectator sees

Casey is the town's resident cold viewer, and the charter designs for his
actual surfaces, in his order of importance:

- **In the world** (headed client, the Orin front room): a street that is
  *occupied* — the carter mid-circuit, a harbour pair hauling, the shop door
  in use, the worksite one stage taller than yesterday. The musters bookend
  the day with everyone visible at once. This is the primary product; every
  other surface is evidence about it.
- **The day record** — one filed page per town day (Thor-side, town
  directory): the rounds completed, transfers confirmed, incidents with
  `fault_owner`, the cost line (§3.3's calls per citizen-day), and the
  crafting shift's changes. Written for a human to skim in two minutes; the
  town's analogue of the round manifest.
- **The twin check** — the fidelity loop's batteries walk the same streets:
  as the builders extend the town, new streets enter the twin protocol
  before they enter the town's rounds (never generate what we cannot judge
  — the twin study's scaling law, applied to growth).

What the spectator must never be shown as a substitute: a dashboard of
self-reported citizen states. Every number on the day record traces to world
evidence or a metered receipt (§3.3, §5).

### 1.5 Growth path (chartered, not launched)

Citizens 9–12 (a second worksite crew; a fisher pair working the harbour
face) are added only when two conditions both hold: the graduation curve
(§3.3) shows the marginal citizen's daily cost at or below the TOWN-2
target, and the twin loop has judged the streets the growth touches. Growth
beyond 12 is a new charter decision, not an implication of this one.

## 2. The citizen stack, mapped onto the town

The Syndicate study's four tiers are a timescale stack, and the town keeps
the stack while re-aiming each tier at the day's shape instead of the round's.
Tier W (world-change) folds into the town's growth and disruption handling
(§6); the three citizen-facing tiers are specified here.

### 2.1 Tier R — reflex scripts (the body layer)

Per citizen: a filed script set on the proven body stack, holding the
citizen's rounds as *compiled-ahead* behaviour — the study's genesis path 1.
A reflex script fires only on **citizen-local state** (the trigger-surface
legality rule, inherited from the spec whole): my position on a surveyed
route, my inventory, the chest in front of me, the day-part bell. A reflex
script never waits on a model call to keep walking, never improvises around
an obstacle it hasn't surveyed, and treats a failed trigger the league's way:
mark, hold, file — the incident is the frame's input, not the body's shame.

Tier R costs **no decode by design** (Addendum 2, §A2.3, carried over):
perception stays reflex-class (the JEV rack rotation pattern is available
where a reflex genuinely needs a perceptual read, and is a rack call, not a
governor call). The body's sparse actor calls — narration, voice, the
character layer's DECLARE/REACT in the Cody lineage — are the only Tier R
spend, and they are capped by the tastes-layer discipline that already
exists (per-hour caps, template-only in the evening; the item-21 overclaim
fix is the standing example of why the caps exist).

### 2.2 Tier D — decision models, per domain (the town's little minds)

Where the spec fields *one* decision engine per syndicate, the town fields
**a small bench of domain decision models**, because a town's decisions are
not one situation picture but several slow ones. Each is a filed rule engine
with at most one bounded model call at its centre (the spec's §2 shape,
including its predictable-default rule: an unthinking Tier D yields a
predictable town, never a frozen one):

- **The roster engine** (day-part scheduling): who works which part, pair
  assignments, muster order. Inputs: the schedule store, yesterday's day
  record, the supply state. Cycle: at each day-part boundary, not continuous.
- **The stock engine** (the economy's referee): shelf levels at the store and
  dock store, delivery intake, restock errand issuance. Admissibility rule
  inherited from the spec's fusion rules: **stock exists only on receiver
  confirmation** — a delivery the receiver hasn't confirmed is not stock, in
  any picture, ever.
- **The works engine** (build sequencing): which build stage is current,
  material readiness from the stock picture, crew assignment via the roster.
  A stage is complete only on survey (world evidence), never on the crew's
  report — phantom digs score zero here exactly as they did there.
- **The social engine** (the town's gatherings and errands of courtesy):
  who visits whom, who carries a message or a gift of stock, the dusk
  muster's pairings. This engine reads the theory-of-mind store (§4) — and
  is deliberately the *last* engine automated, because social scheduling on
  a wrong model of the town produces uncanny citizens faster than any other
  error (§4.3).

Tier D decisions are ~120-token calls on the actor-class serving path; the
decode settlement's corrected arithmetic governs (median 130.2 tok/s
single-stream; a decision ≈ 0.9 s, ~1.5 s at 4-way concurrency) — serving
speed is not the town's constraint at this cast size, and the charter does
not pretend otherwise. The town's constraint is *attention economics*: how
many decisions per day genuinely need a model at all. That is §3's question.

### 2.3 Tier L — the crafting shift (LLMs up high)

Tier L is the town's expensive mind, and its job description is Casey's
sentence made operational: *LLMs up high continually crafting scripts so the
citizens' reflexes get good.* Concretely, per town night, one crafting shift
(the town's reflection, on the spec's reflect pattern — off-clock, a few
large jobs, never during the citizens' day):

1. **Read the day**: the day record, the incident log, the governor receipts,
   the ToM store's accuracy line (§4.2), the cost line.
2. **Improve the reflexes**: revise rounds that failed, widen or narrow
   trigger surfaces, promote a repeatedly hand-guided behaviour into a filed
   script (scaffold fade, with an owner and a clock — the study's §2.4
   discipline), retire dead scripts.
3. **Tune the adjustment level**: how much Tier D may re-plan before it must
   escalate to the shift; how wide each reflex's trigger surface is; the
   roster's pairing policy. The adjustment level is a **filed parameter
   set**, versioned with the town's script book; Tier L is its only editor.
4. **Write the theory of mind**: the day's belief updates, first-pass — the
   layer that starts LLM-written (§4.1) and is progressively displaced by
   structure (§4.2).
5. **File the day's triples**: behaviour/gap/failure triples through the
   commons allocator (`commons/triples/allocate.py` is the only allocation
   path — league law), each failure triple carrying its `fault_owner`.

The crafting shift is also where the *smith* pattern lives: the league's
curriculum stayed smith-authored (operators repair substrate, never write
curriculum), and the town inherits that line — Tier L crafts scripts; it
does not reach into saves, inventories, or the running server.

### 2.4 Governor identities and the expensive-phase budget, stated honestly

The town mints its **own governor identities**, following the spec's pattern
and its launch gate exactly: **every identity exists in the governor config
with a deliberate budget and the correct upstream before TOWN-0**, verified
by config grep plus one metered probe receipt per identity in
`ledger/mind-calls.jsonl`. The permissive default (120 calls / 120
mind-seconds for unmapped keys, failing silently) is a launch-blocking
defect class, full stop — a town body on the default budget presents as a
dumb citizen, and the town will not debug its citizens for a config absence.
Config-write discipline is OPERATIONS §5's: backup first, all identities in
one edit, existing league identities byte-unchanged in the same write,
restart timed before operations begin, never mid-day.

| Identity | Consumer | Upstream | Budget (calls / mind-s per rolling 3600 s) | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `town-<name>` ×8 | Citizen bodies (Tier R voice/routine calls) | `:8311` via `agentUpstreams` | 120 / 300 | The spec's body figure, adopted: sparse voice/routine-shaped calls |
| `town-roster` | Roster engine | `:8311` | 120 / 300 | Boundary-cycle calls only: 3 day-parts + musters ≈ a handful per town day; headroom for re-plans |
| `town-stock` | Stock engine | `:8311` | 120 / 300 | Event-driven (deliveries, shelf crossings); bounded by the town's physical transfer rate |
| `town-works` | Works engine | `:8311` | 120 / 300 | Stage-gated calls; a stage is hours of citizen work per decision |
| `town-social` | Social engine | `:8311` | 60 / 300 | Deliberately the tightest engine budget: social calls are the easiest to waste and the hardest to verify |
| `town-reflect` | Tier L crafting shift | default `:8310` (presence class) | 60 / 1800 | The spec's reflect figure, adopted: a few large jobs per shift, off-clock |
| `town-smith` | Script-crafting seats (the consult-book pattern: provenance-carrying external reads) | books / `:8310` | 60 / 900 | The league's `pulse-smith` figure, adopted for the town's crafting bench |

**What the expensive phase actually costs — the honest arithmetic.** A town
day in the expensive phase (all figures PROPOSAL-grade estimates from the
budgets above and the league's observed call shapes; TOWN-1 replaces them
with receipts):

- **Bodies**: 8 citizens × ~30–50 actor calls per town day (voice, routine
  narration, reflex-adjacent reads) ≈ **240–400 calls per town day**.
- **Tier D**: roster ~10, stock ~20–40 (transfer-paced), works ~10, social
  ~10 ≈ **50–70 model decisions per town day**.
- **Tier L**: one crafting shift ≈ **15–30 large calls** (`:8310`-class,
  long outputs — script revisions are documents, not tokens-in-the-hundreds)
  plus the smith bench's provenance reads.
- **Total: roughly 300–500 metered calls per town day, of which the crafting
  shift is ~5% by count and the majority by mind-seconds and by cost.** Per
  citizen-day: **~40–60 calls**. That is the expensive phase's price, stated
  as the charter's opening position — the number §3 exists to bend. For
  scale honesty: the league's three parked citizens, at their peaks, drew
  comparable per-citizen budgets for *exam* behaviour; the town spends the
  same order per citizen to produce a *status quo*, which is precisely the
  trade Casey ordered — the expense is not the scandal; expense that never
  declines is.

The expensive phase is expected to last **weeks of town days**, not days:
graduation candidacy requires stability windows across independent days and
regimes (§3.1), and a charter that promised a cheap town by Friday would be
selling the photocopy before the original exists.

## 3. The graduation curve — the design's success shape

The town is succeeding exactly insofar as this section's curve bends: **calls
per citizen-day declining at constant liveliness.** Either half alone is a
failure mode with a name — decline without liveliness is the town quietly
becoming furniture; liveliness without decline is the expensive phase become
permanent, a frame that never finds a single QED. The curve is the charter's
headline metric, printed on every day record from TOWN-1 onward.

### 3.1 The pipeline, per behaviour (the design's five stages, town-mapped)

DESIGN-FRAME-TO-WEIGHTS §3.1's stages — accumulation, candidacy, corpus,
exam, adoption/retirement — apply per behaviour, with the town's rung-1
compilation target (filed reflex scripts + structured stores) in place of the
modifier, and the design's tests carried over at full strength:

1. **Accumulation.** The frame (Tier D engine + Tier L shift) produces the
   behaviour deliberatively; every instance is receipted with inputs, the
   state read, and the outcome graded against world evidence. Nothing new —
   this is the town running expensively, with its records kept.
2. **Candidacy.** A behaviour is a candidate when (a) its value is
   demonstrated across a **declared stability window** — a count of
   independent town days fixed in advance, failures inside the window visible
   in the record, not averaged away — and (b) it passes the **state-ablation
   test on its own receipts** (the design's §2.2): replay the decision window
   with live inputs shuffled (right distribution, wrong moment), then frozen
   at window start. Value unchanged → the behaviour was never reading the
   moment; it is a function of the inputs, and it may compile. Value
   collapses → it reads live state; the collapse pattern names the timescale,
   and the behaviour is classified **frame-resident** — a permanent and
   respectable classification, not a rejection. The design's §2.5 pre-run
   detectors ride along: the **knockout replay** (frame-side state zeroed,
   estimating the non-compilable mass the shuffle cannot see, because a proxy
   variable can carry a hidden state-dependence) and the **prober ceiling**
   (a small model predicting the frame's decisions from citizen-visible state
   alone; below ~95% held-out, the family is not compile-eligible — decided
   at probe cost, not at training cost).
3. **Corpus assembly.** The behaviour's receipted instances — **failures
   included at no less than their natural rate**: corrected pairs, declined
   instances, frame errors labelled as such (the design's stage-3 floor,
   adopted verbatim). At rung 1 the "corpus" is the worked example set from
   which the reflex script and its trigger surface are written; the failure
   classes are what teach the script its boundaries.
4. **The graduation exam.** The compiled reflex runs in **shadow** first —
   deciding alongside the frame, its would-be decisions receipted and
   compared, firing nothing — for a declared window; then live, with the
   frame demoted to the alumni role (§3.4). The exam's decisive set is
   **sealed** (days the candidacy selection never touched) **and shifted**
   (a changed regime: a different worksite stage, a supply disruption, a new
   citizen's first week) — because sealed ≠ shifted: sealing protects
   against contamination, not against correlation break, and the town's
   first regime change is scheduled by the game itself (§6) whether we plan
   for it or not.
5. **Adoption and retirement.** At rung 1, adoption is a town governance
   event filed in the day record: the frame stops producing the behaviour,
   the reflex owns it, the alumni checker watches it. **If a behaviour is
   ever proposed for rung 2 — compilation into weights — the design's gate
   applies unchanged and in full: adoption of a persistent weight artefact
   is Casey's, the packet is the design's §3.1 stage-5 packet, and rollback
   is detach.** Nothing in the town's economics urgency amends that gate;
   the silently-uncompilable class (§3.2 below) is the reason it exists.

### 3.2 What compiles first, and what never does

**Expected to graduate first** (input-functions: approximately functions of
what the citizen can see, at decision time):

- **Routes.** Home→square, home→workplace, the carter's circuit, the restock
  path store↔dock. A route's decision content is exhausted by the surveyed
  network and the destination; once walked receipt-clean across a stability
  window, route-following is a reflex with a filed map. (Route *choice under
  disruption* — a blocked street, a moved destination — is not the route; it
  is judgement, and stays up high. The distinction is the spec's THE DOOR
  MOVES lesson, generalised: patching a coordinate is the designed wrong
  answer, in a town exactly as in a round.)
- **Greetings and muster discipline.** Who says what to whom at the square,
  the confirmation formulae, the notice-board read. Format-and-completion
  discipline in the design's typology — the obligation is entirely in the
  situation.
- **Stock-keeping mechanics.** Shelf counts, restock triggers on confirmed
  levels, the till ledger's bookkeeping shape. The *counting* graduates; the
  *admissibility* rule (receiver confirmation) is not a behaviour but a law,
  and laws don't graduate — they are the frame the behaviours run in.
- **Schedule-keeping.** The roster's regular shape: day-part boundaries,
  pair rotations, the muster order. Slow state, compiled as a dated snapshot
  and re-compiled on the roster's own cadence (the design's §2.1
  "approximately" clause, priced honestly).

**Frame-resident forever** (state-functions: they read the live world at
decision time, and compiling them would train an imitation of yesterday's
answers):

- **Novel judgement.** The book-gap: a state no script covers. By definition
  it has no closed form; it lives on scratch paper permanently, correctly.
- **Social repair.** A confirmation refused, a pair that isn't working, a
  citizen whose rounds keep failing for reasons the receipts don't separate.
  Repair reads the town's actual present — beliefs, history, mood of the
  record — and its value collapses under any freeze. It is Tier L's core
  permanent work, and the charter names it as the crafting shift's
  irreducible residue: when everything else has graduated, the shift is a
  social-repair and novelty office, and that is the design working, not
  failing.
- **The verifier, in every form.** The alumni checkers, the census-takers,
  the hold auditors — struck from graduation by the design's §2.4, adopted
  here without discussion: a town whose judge compiled into its citizens
  has no external examiner, and this programme has already met that failure
  wearing other costumes.
- **Anything whose corpus the knockout replay convicts** of hidden
  state-dependence (§3.1 stage 2) — including, pre-registered as the town's
  expected first conviction, **errand selection**: which errand *matters
  now* looks like an input-function and is very probably a proxy for stock
  history and social state the receipts under-record. The charter predicts
  its ablation failure in advance so that the failure, when measured, is a
  confirmation of the instrument — and so that nobody is tempted to compile
  it anyway because the town would be cheaper if it graduated.

### 3.3 Measurement: the curve, and liveliness checks cheaper can't fake

**The cost line.** Per town day, from the governor ledger (a store the
citizens cannot write): metered calls and mind-seconds per identity, rolled
up as **calls per citizen-day** and **mind-seconds per citizen-day**,
printed on the day record beside the expensive-phase baseline (TOWN-1's
measured figure, §2.4's estimate until then). The curve's claim is a trend
across declared windows, never a single day's number.

**Liveliness, checked three ways — none of which the cheaper stack can
produce by being cheaper:**

1. **World-evidence activity census.** Per day, from server state and
   region reads (the mcprobe path — a store the citizens' reporting doesn't
   touch): rounds completed (route traces closing at their destinations),
   transfers confirmed by receivers, build stages advanced on survey, chest
   deltas consistent with the ledger. Liveliness floor, PROPOSAL: ≥85% of
   rostered rounds completed per day, every completed transfer
   receiver-confirmed, ≥1 build stage per two town days. A compiled reflex
   that walks a route to nowhere fails the census exactly as a deliberative
   citizen would — the census does not care which tier produced the act.
2. **The cold-viewer check.** On a declared cadence (PROPOSAL: weekly), a
   viewer who has not read the day records — Casey, or Magnus, or a fleet
   seat shown only the world — watches a muster and a work part and answers
   the twin study's vibe question in town form: *who is that, what are they
   doing, what happens next?* Named-citizen, named-activity answers are the
   pass; "they're just wandering" is a fail that no census number can
   outvote. This is the resident cold test of the twin loop, applied to
   behaviour instead of buildings, and it is the graduation programme's ship
   gate in miniature: **a behaviour whose graduation coincides with a
   cold-viewer decline is un-graduated** (returned to the frame) regardless
   of its exam record.
3. **Seeded disturbances.** On a declared schedule, the town's operators
   plant a small, registered disruption — a stock-out at the store, a
   "closed" sign on a route, a delivery that arrives wrong — sealed-truth
   style (hash filed before the day, revealed at scoring, the adoptions'
   ceremony). The graduated stack must *notice*: the disturbance is either
   handled by a resident frame behaviour (correct: it was never a graduation
   candidate) or escalated by a reflex's declared boundary (correct: the
   boundary works). A disturbance silently absorbed by a compiled behaviour
   doing the *shape* of the right thing — restocking from a shelf that
   isn't there — is the proxy failure observed in the wild, and it reopens
   that behaviour's school on the spot.

**Anti-fake structure, stated plainly:** the cost line can be gamed by
doing less; the census counts only world-side completions; the cold viewer
cannot be briefed by the day record they haven't read; the disturbances are
sealed against the operators' own hindsight. Each check's store is one its
subject cannot write — the Watcher's Charter applied to an economy.

### 3.4 The alumni office, town form

For every graduated behaviour the frame keeps exactly three things (the
design's §3.3, adopted): the behaviour's receipts (the reflex's firings ride
the day record, attributable and priced); **a checker, not the mechanism** —
the cheapest discriminating instrument for that behaviour, of **independent
lineage** (trained/derived from residuals and probe lineages the corpus
never touched; a checker built from the graduate's own corpus is a second
copy of the examined answer); and **the scratch paper** — the candidacy
corpus and windows, kept intact as the re-entry point. Divergence beyond a
declared threshold reopens the school: the reflex is suspended, the frame
resumes the behaviour, and the pipeline re-enters at accumulation. Audit
rates are priced **per application, not per clock** (the design's §3.4
counterweight 4): a behaviour that graduates its way from rare to always-on
is audited at the always-on rate, and that cost is charged against the
graduation's claimed saving on the day record. **The permanent audit tax is
acknowledged in the charter's economics:** the curve in §3.3 is net of
alumni costs, or it is not printed.

## 4. Theory of mind — the town's shared asset

The room-resident-expert design names theory of mind as Track 1 of the rooms
programme — the adjacent frontier. The town is where it stops being adjacent:
a town of eight, running daily, is the smallest system in the fleet whose
citizens *must* model each other to function, and the first whose operation
produces belief-shaped data at a rate worth structuring. Casey's formulation
governs: the data of operation becomes the town's reflexes **and its theory
of mind** — one structuring pipeline, two products.

### 4.1 The belief triple: shape and first contents

A belief is a **filed triple with town fields**, in the commons triples
shape, allocated only through the allocator:

```
subject:    citizen A (believer)
predicate:  does / is-at / holds / will-be-at / can-be-asked-for …
object:     citizen B + the behaviour/place/stock in question
time:       the day-part(s) the belief claims (T)
source:     observed | told-by-<citizen> | shift-inferred | seeded
confidence: the filer's graded estimate, kept uncollapsed
filed:      town day + shift/cycle id
status:     unconfirmed | confirmed | contradicted (by world evidence)
```

Seeded first contents (TOWN-0, hand-filed, labelled `seeded`): the geography
of belief — *the shopkeeper is at the store in work parts; the carter passes
the square every circuit; the senior harbour hand can be asked for dock
stock.* These are the town's common sense, and the charter insists they be
**filed as beliefs with a status**, not baked into scripts as silent
assumptions: a seeded belief that the world contradicts is the ToM layer's
first honest datum, and a script that assumed it silently would instead
produce a confused citizen and a misattributed failure.

### 4.2 The structuring path: LLM-written → consulted structure → reflex input

- **Phase one (the expensive phase): Tier L writes the beliefs.** Each
  crafting shift reads the day and files/updates beliefs — who actually was
  where, whose confirmations actually landed, which junior is now trusted
  with which errand. Beliefs are prose-adjacent triples, generously written,
  expensively maintained. This is correct for phase one: the shift is
  *discovering what the town needs to believe*, and premature structure
  would freeze its guesses.
- **The accuracy line.** Every belief with a checkable claim is scored
  against world evidence on the day record's cadence: *B does X at T* is
  confirmed or contradicted from position traces, transfer receipts, and
  survey — stores the believers don't write. **ToM precision** (confirmed /
  (confirmed + contradicted), per citizen and per predicate family) is
  printed on the day record beside the cost line. A theory of mind that is
  never scored is a fan-fiction file; the scoring is what makes it an asset.
- **Phase two (structuring): beliefs become tables the engines consult.**
  Predicate families whose precision holds above a declared bar across a
  stability window are *compiled into the roster and social engines' input
  tables* — the senior's working hours, the carter's circuit times, the
  store's asking-protocol — still filed, still scored, but now maintained by
  the day's own receipts (a circuit completed updates the circuit belief
  mechanically) rather than by the shift's prose. The shift's ToM work
  narrows to the families that remain imprecise: the novel, the social, the
  changing. This *is* a graduation under §3.1's pipeline — the belief-family
  is the behaviour; the ablation test applies (a belief table that only
  works shuffled is a horoscope, and the test will say so).
- **Phase three (reflex input): the reflexes read the tables.** A reflex
  script's trigger surface may include ToM table reads (the junior goes to
  the store *when the keeper's table says the keeper is there*) — citizen-
  local by file, which keeps the legality rule: the table is part of the
  citizen's filed world, versioned with the script book, and a stale table
  is a filed incident (`fault_owner: prompt`-class — the book was wrong),
  never a citizen's failure of perception.

**Where it lives**: Thor-side, in the town's directory beside the day
records — `tom/` (the triple store), with the commons library as the
cross-programme shape authority and the triples allocator as the only
allocation path. Not in any citizen's context window, not in any model's
weights: the room-resident design's substrate principle — memory as engine
state, single-writer by construction, provenance as the transition log.

### 4.3 What the ToM layer is for, and its standing hazard

Uses, in charter order: the social engine's scheduling (visits and errands
aimed at citizens who will actually be there); the roster's pair policy
(juniors routed to seniors the record says they work well with — where "well"
is confirmations and completions, not sentiment); Tier L's social repair
(§3.2), which is *defined* as work on the gap between the ToM store and the
world; and, at §6, the game's legibility — a syndicate that plays in the
town plays among citizens whose beliefs about each other are filed and
readable, so a disruption's *social* blast radius is measurable.

The hazard, named in advance because the observer cut taught it expensively:
**a belief store can measure the apparatus instead of the town.** If beliefs
are sourced mostly from one engine's filings, their precision measures that
engine's consistency with itself. Counterweights: belief sources are
fielded and reported by mix (a family whose `observed` fraction falls below
its declared floor is downgraded, however precise); the seeded disturbances
(§3.3) include ToM-shaped plants (a citizen's schedule genuinely changed,
unannounced) whose detection is scored in the ToM line, not the cost line;
and the cold-viewer check asks about citizens the viewer can see, so a town
whose paperwork is coherent and whose streets are wrong fails in public.

## 5. Milestones — TOWN-0, TOWN-1, TOWN-2

Each milestone's criteria are **world-evidence criteria**: every line names
its store, and no line is satisfiable by a citizen's, an engine's, or a
lane's self-report. Failure at any line is filed with its failure class and
`fault_owner`; a milestone is claimed only when Muse has verified the
stores first-hand (the standing verification rule: artefact, ledger, tail).

### TOWN-0 — the town exists and the expensive stack is live

- The Sitka-gen district is in place on the town's own server, **mcprobe-
  verified**: the terracing census reads step-face density in the walked
  band (5.4–9.5 per 100 columns) across the town's rounds, in a store the
  generator didn't write.
- Homes row (8 homes), store, dock store, square with notice board, and the
  first worksite are present and surveyed; the street network's routes are
  filed as surveyed routes.
- All 8 citizens spawned as genuine identities, `.dat` sha256s in the town
  manifest, presence re-verified in LIVE immediately before the first
  operating window.
- **All governor identities from §2.4 exist in config** (grep-verified) with
  one metered probe receipt each in the ledger. Any identity on the silent
  default blocks the milestone.
- The non-lethal regime and the daylight hold are **verified against the
  running server** (gamerules/difficulty read live; two consecutive
  refreshes verified as landed in the server log).
- One full muster walked: all 8 citizens home→square→home on surveyed
  routes, **forcedMove count zero** on the Q1a pattern, read from the
  server-side record.

### TOWN-1 — one full town day, receipted

- A complete town day (§1.3) runs end to end: both musters, three work
  parts, the night crafting shift.
- The day record is filed and complete: rounds completed per the census
  (§3.3 check 1), every transfer receiver-confirmed or filed as an incident,
  build state advanced or its non-advance explained by a filed incident.
- **The expensive-phase baseline is measured, not estimated**: calls and
  mind-seconds per citizen-day from the governor ledger, printed on the day
  record as the curve's origin.
- The ToM store holds its seeded beliefs plus one full shift's updates, and
  the first accuracy line is printed — however poor. A TOWN-1 with an
  unscored ToM store has not met this milestone.
- Zero citizen deaths. (Expected by construction under the non-lethal
  regime; stated anyway, because the league's history says unstated
  expectations are the ones that silently lapse.)
- HOLD integrity for the day: every refresh landing-verified, HOLD-LOST
  count zero — or the day's record carries the incident and TOWN-1 is
  re-run, not annotated.

### TOWN-2 — the curve bends

- **First behaviour graduated** through §3.1's full pipeline: candidacy with
  ablation numbers on file, shadow window completed, sealed + shifted exam
  passed, adoption filed, alumni checker live. (Charter expectation, not a
  requirement of identity: a route family or the stock-counting behaviour —
  §3.2's named first class.)
- **The cost line bends**: calls per citizen-day over the declared window
  following the graduation are below the TOWN-1 baseline by at least the
  graduated behaviour's pre-graduation share (the saving is attributed, not
  vibes), **net of the alumni audit cost** (§3.4).
- **Liveliness holds at the cheaper cost**: census floors met, and a cold-
  viewer check inside the window passes at named-citizen/named-activity
  standard. A TOWN-2 claimed on cost alone, with liveliness un-checked in
  the window, is void by this charter's own text.
- At least one seeded disturbance run in the window, detected or escalated
  correctly (§3.3 check 3); an absorbed disturbance reopens the graduated
  behaviour and suspends the milestone claim until re-examined.

### Beyond TOWN-2 (named, not chartered)

TOWN-3 would be the growth gate (§1.5) plus the second graduation class;
the paintball arrival (§6) is gated on TOWN-1 at minimum and designed
against TOWN-2. Both are future charters' work; this one names them so the
programme's shape is visible from here.

## 6. What the paintball game inherits

The game is written up completely and kept ready — the study, Addenda 2/3,
the Pilot A spec, the paint spike's verdicts and fix cycle, the survey
adoptions. Nothing in this charter revises it. What changes, when the town
functions, is what the game is played *in*:

- **A board with a status quo.** Pilot A as specced plays on an empty
  district: the Door, the base, the supply line. A town district has
  rounds running on it — the carter's circuit crosses the play streets, the
  store's stock moves on schedule, citizens hold beliefs about the hour.
  A round played there is a **disruption of a going concern**, and that is
  precisely what makes Tier-W changes legible (the study's A2 doctrine:
  patch at R/D, re-plan at L, re-period at W): THE DOOR MOVES was detectable
  in Pilot A only against the playbook's expectations; in a town, a moved
  door is detectable against *the town's day* — the carter arrives at a
  stock point that isn't there, and the incident is filed by a citizen who
  was not even playing. The registrar gains witnesses it didn't have to
  brief.
- **A cast that already has reflexes.** The town's graduated behaviours are
  the game's background population: syndicates play *among* citizens whose
  routes, greetings and schedules are compiled and therefore predictable —
  the correct kind of predictable, the kind a strategy can be built against
  — while the town's frame-resident behaviours (repair, judgement) keep the
  background from being furniture.
- **An economy with receipts.** Paint supply in the spec is a station and a
  charge count; in the town it can be *stock* — made, hauled, confirmed,
  occasionally out — inside an economy whose admissibility rule (receiver
  confirmation) the game already adopted. The Vault Door objective gains a
  supply chain with a history.
- **A fidelity-judged place.** The twin loop's Lincoln Street battery and
  its successors run on the same streets the game uses; the game's board
  arrives pre-judged for recognition and vibe, which the empty-district
  pilot would have had to take on faith.

**Arrival gate**: the game's Pilot A Phase 0 may not begin until TOWN-1 is
verified (a full day receipted) — and the charter records the reason in
Casey's terms: the game was commissioned as a disruption of a world, and
until the town functions there is no world-status-quo to disrupt, only an
arena to occupy. Pilot A's empty-district form remains valid and ready as
the fallback if the town slips; the preference, not the possibility, is
what the gate expresses.

## 8. Open questions, each with its cheapest settling experiment

1. **Town-day length.** Is ~50 real minutes the right day (long enough for
   rounds to matter, short enough for a crafting shift per day)? *Cheapest
   test*: TOWN-1 runs at the proposed length; the day record's incident mix
   (rushed rounds vs idle citizens) prices the error in one day.
2. **Does the stock economy generate errands at a living rate**, or does the
   town idle between deliveries? *Cheapest test*: the supply rule's delivery
   cadence is a filed parameter; TOWN-1's census shows rounds-completed vs
   rounds-issued, and an idle gap >1 day-part is the signal to re-tune —
   measured, not guessed in advance.
3. **Rung-1 compilation fidelity.** When a route graduates from Tier D
   guidance to a filed reflex, how much of the frame's gain survives?
   *Cheapest test*: the first graduation's shadow window *is* the
   measurement (§3.1 stage 4); the charter pre-registers the shadow
   comparison so the number exists whether or not it flatters.
4. **ToM precision's honest ceiling.** What fraction of checkable beliefs
   can a phase-one shift actually keep confirmed? *Cheapest test*: TOWN-1's
   first accuracy line; the charter deliberately sets no bar before the
   first measurement exists.
5. **The carter as single point of circulation.** One carter is the town's
   simplest design and its obvious bottleneck. *Cheapest test*: the day
   record's transfer latency (delivery→confirmation) across TOWN-1's
   successors; a rising latency at constant stock is the case for the second
   carter, made by receipts.
