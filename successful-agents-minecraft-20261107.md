# What Already Works: Successful Survival-Game Agents, Graded

A sceptical survey of agents that genuinely succeed at Minecraft-style survival play in the world — what each one proved, how strong the proof is, which mechanism transfers, and whether that mechanism fits the Craftmind league (scripted character loops + a small local actor + slow seat libraries, world-evidence verdicts only).

The short version: the field is very good at **tech-tree climbing inside fresh episodes** and **short-horizon instruction following**. It is weak to nonexistent at **living in one persistent world** — surviving nights under mob pressure, recovering from death, maintaining a base over days, building a town from gathered materials. Almost every headline number below comes from an episode that resets. The league's hardest problems (S5 night survival, memory across deaths, town building) are precisely the ones the published field has *not* solved, which is both a warning and an opening: there is no incumbent to copy there, only mechanisms to steal from the episode world.

Evidence grades used throughout:

- **A** — Public competition or benchmark, multi-episode, scored by parties other than the authors, artefacts public.
- **B** — Peer-reviewed or widely scrutinised paper, controlled multi-trial benchmark run by the authors, ablations reported.
- **C** — Authors' case study, human-judged small-sample evaluation, or demo with partial quantification.
- **D** — Cherry-picked video, social post, or marketing claim with no denominator.

## Per-system sections

### Voyager — the skill library is real; the lifestyle is not

**Claim and evidence.** Voyager (Wang et al., NVIDIA/Caltech et al., arXiv:2305.16291) is a GPT-4 agent on Mineflayer that obtained 3.3× more unique items than ReAct, Reflexion and AutoGPT re-implemented in the same stack, travelled 2.3× farther, unlocked wood/stone/iron tiers 15.3×/8.5×/6.4× faster in prompting iterations, discovered 63 unique items in 160 iterations, and was the only baseline to reach the diamond tier — in one of three runs, at ~102 iterations. With its learned library moved to a *new world with empty inventory*, it solved four unseen tasks (diamond pickaxe, golden sword, lava bucket, compass) in 3 of 3 trials each while baselines solved none in 50 iterations. Ablations are the strongest part of the paper: random curriculum −93% items, no self-verification −73%, GPT-3.5 instead of GPT-4 for code 5.7× fewer items. Sources: https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2305.16291 ; https://aiunderstanding.org/learn/voyager-and-skill-library-agents

**What failed, in the paper's own telling.** The critic produces false negatives (it once rejected a successful spider kill because it didn't recognise string as the drop). The curriculum hallucinates impossible goals ("copper sword"). The code writer invents APIs and bad physics (cobblestone as fuel). The agent gets stuck. GPT-4 cost ~15× GPT-3.5 and the paper reports no total run cost; independent write-ups estimate $50–200 per 160-iteration run. Critically, Voyager's world interaction runs through Mineflayer's privileged API — it perceives structured state, not pixels — and its "life" is a sequence of prompting iterations in episodes, not days survived under pressure. Its celebrated structure builds (a house, a Nether portal) happened **with a human acting as critic or curriculum provider**, not autonomously. Sources: https://github.com/kannguyen1/agent-research-book/blob/HEAD/docs/89-voyager-deep.md ; https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2305.16291

**Mechanism.** Three parts, all load-bearing per the ablations: (1) an *automatic curriculum* that proposes the next task from current inventory/biome/completed-and-failed lists, pitched at feasible novelty; (2) *iterative code generation* — write a Mineflayer program, run it, feed back execution errors + environment messages + a separate critic's verdict, revise up to ~4 rounds; (3) a *skill library* — only programmes that pass verification are stored, indexed by an embedding of a natural-language description, retrieved top-5 for future tasks and composed. Learning is entirely in the library, never in weights.

**League mapping.** This is the closest published relative of the league's own System-3 design, and it validates the split the league already runs: slow strong models write and repair code (the seat libraries), a cheap loop executes it, a verifier gates what enters the library. It speaks directly to crafting chains (verified code, not re-prompted hope) and to navigation wedges (a wedged skill is a *failed verification with an error trace*, revised or discarded, never retried identically). It does **not** speak to survival under pressure or memory across deaths — Voyager's library persists, but its body and world stakes do not. Fit: excellent, with one adaptation — Voyager's critic is an LLM judging traces; the league's stricter rule (verdicts from world state: inventory contents, block scans, server logs) is the better verifier and should be kept. Voyager's numbers also warn what happens without it: remove verification and discovery collapses by 73%.

### VPT — scale bought a motor system, not a mind

**Claim and evidence.** OpenAI's Video PreTraining (Baker et al., NeurIPS 2022) trained an inverse-dynamics model on a small labelled contractor set, used it to label ~70,000 hours of online Minecraft video, and behaviour-cloned a foundation policy that plays at 20 Hz through the native keyboard/mouse interface. Zero-shot it already crafts planks and crafting tables (~1,000-action sequences). Behaviour-cloning fine-tune reaches stone tools; RL fine-tune crafts diamond pickaxes in **2.5% of 10-minute episodes**, with human-level success on every precursor item — the first reported diamond-tool craft by an agent. A separate fine-tune case study had contractors play "build a house in 10 minutes" and produced a model that builds simple houses from basic materials (case-study grade, no success rate published). Sources: http://openai.com/index/vpt/?ref=vgtimes ; https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.11795

**Sceptic's notes.** The headline numbers conflate tasks across papers and must be kept apart: "obtain a diamond" (VPT scores 20% in GITM's comparison table, a different setup), "craft a diamond pickaxe from scratch in 10 minutes" (2.5%, OpenAI's own RL result), "diamond pickaxe reliability" (JARVIS-1 quotes VPT at 2.5% and itself at up to 12.5% in its own setup). The 2.5% is the honest one: even with 70k hours of prior, the full chain succeeds one episode in forty. RL from scratch, same paper, essentially never collects logs — the prior is doing nearly all the work. Cost: the contractor/labelling programme was reported at ~$160k, before compute. Sources: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.17144 ; https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2311.05997

**Mechanism.** Buy competence at the *bottom* of the stack: a general behavioural prior over low-level control (movement, camera, GUI manipulation, combat timing) learned from massive imitation, then cheap specialisation on top. Nothing in VPT plans, remembers, or reasons about goals — its successors (STEVE-1, JARVIS-1's controllers) exist precisely to bolt a mind onto the motor system.

**League mapping.** The league cannot and should not replicate 70k hours of video — but it already owns the same architectural insight in script form: Mineflayer's pathfinder and the citizens' proven low-level primitives (goto, dig-with-stand-closure, collect) *are* the behavioural prior, hand-built instead of learned. VPT's lesson for the league is about where failures live: VPT agents fail at long-horizon *sequencing*, almost never at "can the body swing the pick." The league's trial history rhymes — bodies walk, chains break. Spend on the sequencing/verification layer (System 2/3), not on re-solving motor control. VPT also sets the honesty bar for diamond-tier claims: 2.5% with that prior is what "success" actually looked like; any league citizen reaching iron reliably in a persistent world is already operating in rare territory.

### STEVE-1 and the VPT-controller family — instruction following, short horizons only

**Claim and evidence.** STEVE-1 (Lifshitz et al., 2023) fine-tunes VPT with a MineCLIP-based text-conditioning bridge so the policy follows short text instructions ("collect wood", "dig down"). On atomic-task benchmarks its success is real but modest — many task groups sit between 20% and 75%, and later independent tables (JARVIS-VLA's MCU comparison) put STEVE-1's group averages around 0.3–0.6, comparable to VPT variants, well below the newer VLA models. Successors (GROOT, ROCKET-1, JARVIS-VLA, OmniJARVIS) steadily improve *short-horizon, spatially-grounded* interaction — ROCKET-1 reports 0.82–0.95 on a 12-task interaction benchmark — but all remain episode-scoped atomic skills. Sources: https://pdfs.assets.alphaxiv.org/2503.16365v2.pdf ; https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025/papers/Cai_ROCKET-1_Mastering_Open-World_Interaction_with_Visual-Temporal_Context_Prompting_CVPR_2025_paper.pdf

**Mechanism.** Condition a strong motor prior on language (or on visual-temporal prompts) so a planner can *call* behaviour in words instead of code.

**League mapping.** Low direct fit — the league's citizens call Mineflayer functions, not neural policies, and a 0.6B local actor cannot run a VLA. The transferable idea is the *interface discipline*: a small, fixed vocabulary of named, parameterised skills that a planner composes, with per-skill success rates measured and published. That is exactly what the league's smith-authored curriculum plus verified skill shelf should look like from the actor's chair: the actor picks from a menu whose prices (success rates) are known from world evidence.

### MineRL Diamond competitions — the control group for everyone's claims

**Claim and evidence.** The NeurIPS MineRL ObtainDiamond competitions are the field's reality check because organisers, not authors, held the stopwatch. In 2019, ~660 entries: **none obtained a diamond**; first place (Skrynnik et al.) went to a hierarchical RL system using human demonstrations as priors, ranked on intermediate-item score. In 2021, the relaxed intro track finally produced competition diamonds — WinOrGoHome obtained diamond in 21 of 100 evaluation episodes after ~625M frames (2.5B across runs) with a hand-designed 20-action space and action masking; the stricter research track's results were explicitly "not directly comparable" and did not solve the task in the generalisable sense organisers wanted. Sources: https://tviskaron.github.io/Alexey_Skrynnik_CV.pdf ; https://pub-fad10fba280c45dba0823fd585acc08c.r2.dev/pdf/60898e19e3d9747c0a780a625405713d49a75abf.pdf?v=1789164144

**Mechanism.** What the winners shared: hierarchy (sub-policies per tech-tree stage), demonstrations used as priors rather than the whole solution, and aggressive action-space engineering to make exploration tractable. What they also shared: frame budgets in the hundreds of millions for a single narrow task.

**League mapping.** Two lessons. First, calibration: if organised competition with demo datasets and GPU-weeks produced *zero* diamonds in 2019 and 21% in a relaxed 2021 track, then LLM-agent papers claiming 67.5% (GITM) are measuring an easier thing — structured actions and privileged state — and should be read that way (see GITM below). Second, the winning shape — stage decomposition with a specialist per stage — is the league's S1–S5 exam structure already. The competitions validate staged curricula with per-stage scoring over monolithic "play well" objectives.

### MineRL BASALT — the only verified "agent built a house" evidence, with asterisks

**Claim and evidence.** BASALT (NeurIPS 2021, retrospective 2022; second edition 2022) gave agents four reward-free tasks described only in English — FindCave, MakeWaterfall, CreateVillageAnimalPen, BuildVillageHouse — and scored submitted agents by **human pairwise judgement** of recorded runs (TrueSkill). KAIROS won 2021 overall and the most-humanlike prize with a hybrid: behaviour cloning for navigation plus a hand-labelled image classifier (88,000 annotations over 200+ videos) steering task state. In 2022, GoUp won with the most league-shaped system of the era: each task split into "parts a person can turn into code" (scripts + a finite-state machine) and "parts that need ML" (a fine-tuned VPT mover, a YOLOv5 animal detector, a MobileNet fence-placement detector). The houses and pens in the winning videos exist — humans watched them being built and ranked them best. Sources: https://prx.oniket.workers.dev/:443/https/proceedings.mlr.press/v176/shah22a/shah22a.pdf ; https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.13512 ; https://www.engineering.columbia.edu/about/news/columbia-engineering-students-win-1st-place-neurips-minecraft-challenge

**Sceptic's notes.** The asterisks matter: agents spawn *in a village, with a starting inventory*, on an episode clock. Nobody gathered the materials under survival pressure, nobody defended the build, nobody maintained it, and "best" is a relative TrueSkill rank among weak entrants, not an objective completion certificate. This is the strongest "agent built a functioning structure" evidence in the literature and it is still an exam, not a life.

**Mechanism.** Hybrid decomposition by *solvability class*: script the deterministic spine, learn only the perceptual/motor gaps, glue with an explicit state machine. Pure end-to-end entries lost to hybrids in both editions.

**League mapping.** Direct hit for town building. The league's ensure-style project predicates are GoUp's state machine wearing different clothes; BASALT says that architecture beats end-to-end learning for construction, and that the winning spend is on *perception of the specific build site* (where are the animals? where does the fence line go?) rather than on a smarter planner. For the league: surveyed build sites (the cell-survey pattern already proven on Cody's shoulder) plus scripted placement plus world-scan verification is the BASALT-winning shape. It also speaks to verdicts: when a task has no programmatic success condition ("a good pen"), BASALT used structured human judgement — the league's analogue is the curator + dock review, and BASALT's pairwise-comparison method is a cheap way to make that judgement less vibes-based.

### MineDojo and MineCLIP — the judge is the product

**Claim and evidence.** MineDojo (Fan et al., NeurIPS 2022 outstanding paper) is infrastructure: a simulator with 3,142 tasks (1,581 programmatic with ground-truth success checks; 1,560 creative with none; 1 playthrough — defeat the Ender Dragon, still essentially unclaimed), plus a knowledge base of 730k YouTube videos, 6k wiki pages and 340k Reddit posts. Its agent contribution, MineCLIP, is a video-language model trained on that corpus and used as a *learned reward*: an RL agent rewarded by MineCLIP was competitive with hand-designed rewards on a 12-task test and up to +73% on individual tasks, and MineCLIP doubles as an automatic scorer for creative tasks whose correlation with human scores the paper reports. Sources: https://docs.minedojo.org/sections/core_api/sim.html ; https://the-decoder.com/minedojo-and-the-unreasonable-effectiveness-of-data/

**Mechanism.** When you cannot write the success function, *learn the judge* from internet-scale human footage and text, and use the same judge for training reward and for evaluation.

**League mapping.** The league already enforces the programmatic half (world-evidence verdicts). MineCLIP is the missing tool for the other half — the creative/town layer where "is this a town?" has no block-scan answer. The league's JEV rack is advisor-only and must stay that way for gates, but a MineCLIP-style learned scorer is the honest candidate for *ranking* builds, reels and settlement progress where the dock currently has only human taste. Also note the knowledge-base pattern: GITM and Voyager both feed on wiki text; the league's consult books are a private, provenance-stamped version of the same idea — MineDojo says the public corpus is worth plumbing in as a *reference shelf* for the seats, clearly separated from verified local knowledge.

### GITM — the strongest numbers, the softest ground

**Claim and evidence.** Ghost in the Minecraft (Zhu et al., ICLR 2024; arXiv:2305.17144) reports 67.5% success on ObtainDiamond — +47.5 points over VPT's 20% in its table — 100% on crafting table and wooden/stone pickaxe milestones, 95% on iron pickaxe, and non-zero success on **all 262 Overworld items**, framed as "the first to procure all items / unlock the entire technology tree," with no GPU training (one 32-core CPU node). Sources: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.17144 ; https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OpenGVLab/GITM/main/README.md

**Sceptic's notes.** This is the survey's most over-claimed success (see the closing note). Three compressions hide in the headline. (1) "Unlocked the entire tech tree" aggregates *per-item success rates across separate task episodes* — the agent was asked for each item individually, often with the prerequisites effectively staged by the task setup; no single run, body or world accumulated all 262. (2) The 67.5% is achieved through *structured actions*: the LLM plans over a hand-built action vocabulary executed by scripted controllers, which removes exactly the navigation, timing and recovery failures that dominate real play (and dominate the league's death ledger). (3) There is no independent replication, and the comparison VPT number (20%) is GITM's own table. None of this makes GITM useless — its decomposition discipline is genuinely good — but its numbers measure the planning layer in isolation, not a surviving agent.

**Mechanism.** A three-level LLM hierarchy: a Decomposer turns a goal into sub-goals using text knowledge harvested from the wiki (recipes, prerequisites); a Planner turns sub-goals into structured actions; an Interface grounds actions in controls. A text memory stores goals and outcomes so the planner can re-plan around failures instead of restarting. Knowledge is *supplied*, not learned from pixels.

**League mapping.** The league's seats already play Decomposer/Planner; the steal is GITM's insistence that the knowledge layer be **explicit text** — recipe/prerequisite graphs the planner reads verbatim — rather than whatever the model happens to remember. The league has been bitten by exactly this (a smith inventing recipes; fact patches hand-fed to a prompt). A league-owned, versioned recipe/fact graph, consulted by every seat take and every curriculum script, is GITM's real gift. The mapping to survival is nil: GITM's agent does not manage hunger, night or death in any reported way.

### Plan4MC and RL-GPT — honest numbers, useful shape

**Claim and evidence.** Plan4MC (Yuan et al., 2023) pre-trains basic RL skills, builds a *skill graph*, and has an LLM plan paths through it: across 40 MineDojo tasks needing 25–121 planning steps it averages 0.417 / 0.293 / 0.267 / 0.320 success on its four task sets — it "accomplishes all tasks" only in the sense of non-zero success, and its ablations show the dedicated **find-skill** (learned object search) is load-bearing. RL-GPT (2024) splits tasks between GPT-written code policies and RL-trained residual skills, reaching ObtainDiamond at 8% with 3M environment samples against VPT's 20% at 16.8B — less successful, ~5,000× cheaper in samples. Sources: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.16563 ; https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.19299

**Mechanism.** Plan over a graph of *named skills with known preconditions and success rates*; give search ("find X") first-class skill status instead of hoping the planner's movement policy stumbles onto things.

**League mapping.** Plan4MC's find-skill speaks to the league's navigation wedges and collect failures: citizens rarely fail because the plan was wrong; they fail because "go to the oak" hides a search-and-approach problem with its own failure modes (unreachable columns, canopy headroom, protection squares). Promoting *find/approach* to a verified skill with its own evidence (stand cell surveyed, distance ≤ reach, displacement confirmed) — which the league's stand-closure fix already prototypes — is Plan4MC's lesson applied. RL-GPT's split is the league's existing seat/loop split with the accounting to prove it: code for what code does well, learning/effort only for the residual.

### JARVIS-1 — memory of *its own* successes

**Claim and evidence.** JARVIS-1 (Wang et al., 2023; arXiv:2311.05997) couples a multimodal planner to VPT-family controllers and reports near-perfect performance on entry/intermediate tasks across 200+ Minecraft Universe Benchmark tasks, and up to 12.5% on the long-horizon ObtainDiamondPickaxe task — five times VPT's 2.5% in its setup. Its ablations attribute the long-horizon gain substantially to its **multimodal memory**: a store of (visual situation, task, successful plan) entries from the agent's own prior play, retrieved at planning time; the paper also shows a self-improvement loop where memory accumulated in earlier tasks raises later success. Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.05997v1 ; https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2311.05997

**Sceptic's notes.** "Nearly perfect across 200+ tasks" covers mostly short-horizon obtain/craft tasks with staged starting conditions; the flagship long-horizon number is 12.5%, i.e. the best mind-plus-memory of 2023 still failed seven diamond-pickaxe episodes in eight. The memory result is nonetheless the paper's durable contribution because the ablation isolates it.

**Mechanism.** Episodic memory as retrieval, not narrative: store the *situation signature* alongside the plan that worked, and let future planning start from the nearest proven plan instead of from first principles.

**League mapping.** This is the direct answer to the league's memory-across-deaths problem. A citizen's body and inventory reset on death; its library must not. JARVIS-1 says the unit to store is not a diary entry but a *(situation → plan → verified outcome)* triple — for the league: (terrain/survey signature + project + the exact script revision that passed, with its world-evidence verdict). Cody's surveyed shoulder route is precisely one such triple, currently buried in a ledger. Promote those triples into the consult books with epoch stamps (the ProArt critique's prior-epoch warning applies verbatim) and the next citizen — or the same citizen after death — starts from the nearest proven plan. It also speaks to the two-community design: each community's book becomes its JARVIS-memory, and divergence between books becomes measurable culture.

### The town question — what "verified" would even look like

No system in this survey — and none found beyond it — demonstrates an agent that, in a persistent survival world, gathered materials under pressure, built a functioning base, *maintained and used it across days and deaths*, and extended it toward a town, with public evidence. The nearest exhibits, in descending order of strength: BASALT's human-judged village houses and pens (episode-scoped, gifted inventory and village — Grade A− as a competition, C as evidence about living); VPT's house-building fine-tune case study (real learned behaviour, no published success rate — Grade C); Voyager's human-in-the-loop structures (the human was the curriculum or the critic — Grade C as autonomy evidence); and a long tail of social-media build videos with no denominators (Grade D — e.g. individual "AI builds a house" reels whose code exists but whose failure runs are uncounted: https://www.instagram.com/reel/DdS9gLjB4dA/). The league's own S3/S4 (shelter with door and torch; chest stocked inside it, verified by scan) is, on this evidence, already a stricter functioning-base test than most of the published field has passed in the open. A *town* — multiple maintained structures, paths, storage logistics, more than one citizen — has no incumbent. Whoever does it first, with receipts, sets the benchmark.

## Steal these five mechanisms first (ranked for the league)

1. **Voyager's verified skill library, with the league's stricter verifier.** Only programmes that pass a check enter the library; the library is embedding-indexed by description and retrieved at task time. League adaptation: the check is world evidence (inventory delta, block scan, server log), not an LLM critic — Voyager's own ablation (−73% without verification) plus the critic's documented false negatives make the upgrade obvious. *Fits:* seats write/repair skills slowly, the loop executes them, the dock admits them. Speaks to: crafting chains, wedge recovery (a failed skill returns with an error trace and is revised, never re-run identically), and cross-citizen transfer (Vega inherits Cody's proven skills — with provenance).
2. **JARVIS-1's (situation → plan → outcome) episodic triples.** Store surveyed-terrain signatures with the exact plan revision that worked and its verdict; retrieve the nearest triple before planning. *Fits:* pure bookkeeping in the consult books/ledger; no model changes. Speaks to: memory across deaths (the triples live outside the body), navigation (shoulder routes, protection-square exits, corridor maps become retrievable assets), and the two-community experiment (each book's triples are its culture, countable).
3. **BASALT/GoUp's solvability-class decomposition for building.** Script the deterministic spine (placement from surveys), reserve learning/judgement for the perceptual gaps (site selection, animal/fence detection analogues: canopy, headroom, stand cells), glue with explicit project state. *Fits:* it is the character loop's current shape, now with competition pedigree; the new spend is site perception — pre-build surveys as a standard project step. Speaks to: hut completion, the town ring, and pen-building (the league's own next projects are BASALT's 2021 task list, in order).
4. **GITM's explicit text knowledge graph for recipes and prerequisites.** A league-owned, versioned fact base (recipes by plank *type*, furnace fuels, protection rules, reach distances) that seats and smiths must read, replacing prompt-fact patches and model memory. *Fits:* a file, not a model; seats already consult books. Speaks to: the crafting-chain failures that cost Vega 352 attempts and Cody several trials — nearly all were *knowledge* failures (wrong recipe shape, wrong assumptions) wearing the clothes of motor failures.
5. **Plan4MC's first-class find/approach skill, with published per-skill success rates.** Split every "gather X" into find (search + surveyed stand cell) and harvest, measure each skill's success rate from world evidence, and let the actor/curriculum see the price list. *Fits:* the stand-closure and surveyed-hop machinery already exists in prototype; this is promotion and accounting, not new invention. Speaks to: navigation wedges, collect loops, and honest curriculum choice (a citizen should not be assigned a chain whose weakest skill is at 20% without the plan saying so).

## Closing note: the most over-claimed success

**GITM's "first to unlock the entire technology tree."** The sentence invites the reader to picture an agent that played Minecraft up the whole tree. The measurement underneath is a matrix of per-item success rates from separate, staged task episodes, executed through scripted structured actions that abstract away the navigation, combat-timing and recovery problems that constitute most of actual survival play — and its flagship 67.5% has never been independently replicated, while the organised-competition control group (MineRL) shows how the same task behaves when outsiders hold the stopwatch and the action space is honest: zero diamonds in 2019, 21% in a relaxed track in 2021. GITM's decomposition and explicit-knowledge mechanisms are worth stealing (they are steal #4). Its headline is a planning-layer result marketed as a playing result. The league's own doctrine — succeeded on a benchmark is not succeeded at living in a world — exists for exactly this sentence.
