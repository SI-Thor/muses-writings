## 0. What this document is

### 0.1 The fallback's place in the programme

The town charter (§6) set the arrival gate: Pilot A Phase 0 not before **verified TOWN-1**, because the game was commissioned as a disruption of a world and until the town functions there is no status quo to disrupt. The same section keeps Pilot A's **empty-district form valid and ready as the fallback if the town slips**. The spec itself (§7, its board note) already carries the seed of the fallback: *if generation slips, a graded-flat copy of existing terrain is the fallback board and Sitka follows for Pilot B.*

This document is that fallback written to execution completeness — the same discipline the programme applied to the district form: every phase, gate, budget and verdict pre-registered, so that invoking it is a decision, not a design session. It exists so that a Sitka-gen slip, a WP-A send-back, or a deliberate cheap-first choice never leaves the programme improvising its flagship experiment under time pressure — improvisation under time pressure is how pre-registration dies.

### 0.2 The one structural difference, and the enumerated deltas

The fallback runs the **identical** pilot — same cast, same objective, same two-round shape, same criteria — on a **purpose-built flat test world** instead of the Sitka-gen district. Everything else is inherited. The complete list of differences from the spec:

### 0.3 What the fallback is for, in the programme's own terms

Three uses, in charter order: **(a)** insurance — if the district slips or is sent back, the Syndicate loop (playbook → round → census → reflection → sealed-change calibration) is still provable this season, on the record, instead of waiting on terrain; **(b)** a cheaper first exercise of every Pilot A mechanism *except* terrain — the tiers, the governor gate, the census referee, the seal — so the district run, when it comes, spends its surprises on the district; **(c)** a control: the same pilot on two boards is the programme's cleanest substrate-vs-script separator, because a failure that repeats on flat ground was never the terrain's fault. What it is **not** for is stated in §10 (non-goals): it produces no town evidence, no district evidence, and no Pilot B argument by itself.

## 1. The board — a graded-flat test world

### 1.1 Construction

A scratch Paper server (fleet bundle, Paper 26.1.2 build 74, Java 25+ — the spec §1.1 construction, adopted whole), Thor-hosted, own directory and **own booked ports** (game + RCON recorded in the agent-inbox booking at execution time; ports are booked, never assumed free — the paint spike's RCON-collision lesson). The world is a **flat test world**: a levelled plain at a single surface height (PROPOSAL: surface y 64 over a plain of grass/dirt on stone, sea-level convention irrelevant — there is no water in play), generated flat by construction (superflat-style preset or an equivalent flattening pass over a generated seed — the mechanism is the executing lane's choice; the *evidence* is §1.5's probe, not the preset's promise).

The board's extent is sized to the game, not to a town: PROPOSAL — a fenced working area of roughly **600 × 300 blocks** (base at the west end, Door site A centred ~200 blocks east, Door site B ~400 blocks east), the fence being a scenario boundary (a marked line the playbook treats as the world edge), not a claim about the world's size. Outside the fence the flat world continues, unplayed and unsurveyed.

### 1.2 The monument, base, and stations

- **The Vault Door (site A).** Exactly the spec §4 monument: a flat-faced structure **9 wide × 12 high** of a light neutral block on a graded pad (the pad is trivial on flat ground — it is the monument's footprint, level with the plain), its face divided into **4 sectors A–D**, the same sectors the builder's plays work — scoring grid and working grid are one grid. The monument's coordinates, orientation and sector boundaries are filed in the arena survey (§1.4) and in the sealed change file (§7.2) before Round 1.
- **Base anchor.** The syndicate's surveyed anchor at the west end: spawn point, the **pigment station** (refills a splat gun's charge instantly on attendance — Spike 2's contract), chest **C-BASE** (named, published coordinates).
- **Chest C-DOOR.** The named cache chest at the Door, per the NOOR play's binding: placed at a filed offset from the monument (close enough to attend between sectors, outside the census face).
- **The route.** Base ↔ Door over open flat ground, marked per RH-1 (mark-then-move) during the survey: waypoints filed in the arena survey with hop spacing audited against the harness's arrival radius (the league's inherited fix: waypoints inside the gotoNear radius count as consumed — route authors audit spacing regardless, spec §1.3).

### 1.3 Paint (Spike 2's layer, unchanged contract)

The fallback loads the same paint layer the district form does; the spike is **complete** (booking record, Thor `~/syndicate-paint/`: persistence PASS, census referee PASS, HIT-OUT + walk-back verified with world evidence, PAINT.md §§8–9). This plan requires of it exactly the spec §4 contract: **(a)** splats server-authoritative and persistent across the round (and across a restart — the checklist's item 4); **(b)** the census reports per-sector coverage fraction from world state; **(c)** every splat event logged with shooter, position and time (the firing log's paint half). Splat guns hold a finite charge (spec PROPOSAL: **8 large splats per charge**); a carried charge is inventory, hence NOOR-transferable via the named chests.

### 1.4 The arena survey (filed before the script phase)

Contents, mirroring the spec §7.1 Phase 0 list, flat-board form: base anchor coordinates; Door site A coordinates + sector grid; Door site B coordinates (surveyed *as a site* — pad position and route — but its monument is built only by the seeded change; see §7.2's honesty note); C-DOOR / C-BASE coordinates; pigment station position; the base↔A route with waypoint spacing audit; relief statement (class 0–1 by construction, probe-verified per §1.5); the fence line. The survey is the playbook's only legal geography: Tier L re-derives every playbook coordinate from it, and any line it cannot ground in the survey is cut (spec Phase 1 rule, unchanged).

### 1.5 Board acceptance (the fallback's Phase 0 terrain gate)

Three checks, all from stores the board's builder doesn't write:

1. **Flatness probe.** A mcprobe read (or equivalent region read) over the working area: surface height constant within ±0 blocks across the fenced area except the monument, stations and chests themselves; any deviation is a build defect, fixed and re-probed. (This is the walked-band census's honest flat-board analogue: the question is not "is the relief gentle" but "is the board the board the plan says it is".)
2. **Monument check.** Door dimensions, sector grid and neutral facing verified against §1.2 by direct read of the built structure.
3. **Census calibration.** A hand-painted test sector's coverage reproduced by the census within **±5 points** (the spec's checklist item 4, unchanged) — the referee is calibrated before it is trusted, on this board, not inherited from the spike's bench.

## 3. The objective and its referee

**The one objective (spec §4, unchanged):**

> **By the horn, cover at least 60% of the Vault Door's face in the syndicate colour (marigold), as measured by the paint census over the server-side world state.**

Why 60% (spec, restated): the paint-native analogue of the study's bank-≥75%-of-manifest criterion — coverage is a fraction of a surveyed whole, receiver-side (the census reads the world, not the citizens), set below the resource figure because paint pays a logistics tax (charges are finite, refill only at base). The value is **pre-registered before the drop and unchangeable after it**; if it proves mis-set, that is Round 1's filed lesson for a future scenario, never a mid-flight edit.

**Census discipline.** The census runs in full at the horn (scoring) and in lightweight sector reads continuously for the builder (a *local* read of server state). Tier D receives **at most two** full-census snapshots mid-round, on its t ≡ 17 (mod 20 s) offset — rationed so the census stays a referee and does not become a steering wheel. **No self-report scores anything**: scoring reads the census, the firing log, the picture journal, the governor ledger and the server log.

**Tagging in a vs-world round.** No opponent syndicate: the tag-out rule is exercised in its world form — a citizen whose charge is exhausted away from a chest performs the **SO-3 walk-back** (stop work, walk the marked route to base, reset at the pigment station, return). The walk *is* the penalty, priced in coverage time; there is no bench for walk-backs. Deaths remain possible from the world itself and are governed by §5.3's circuit-breaker — paint dissolved the opponent-inflicted grind, not gravity.

## 4. Tier D — the captain's decision engine (spec §2, restated to execute)

One engine, wearing the captain hat functionally, running Thor-side beside the governor, holding Marigold's Tier D client. A **filed decision-rule engine with one bounded model call at its centre** — not a free-running LLM.

**The situation picture** — assembled every cycle from exactly six inputs, all stores or signals the engine does not author: **(1)** citizen state snapshots (position, hp, food, inventory, current play + step, last call) from the harness state files, read never inferred; **(2)** reflex products accumulated over the last 60 s (contact-class only — `splat-nearby`, `colour-bearing:<colour>:<bearing>`, `obstacle:<bearing>`, `hat-signal:<hat>`) — counted, not interpreted; a colour-bearing sighting enters only on **two independent reports** (two bodies, or one body twice ≥10 s apart); **(3)** the play-state ledger (plays live / fired / aborted / completed, from the script engine's firing log); **(4)** the supply picture from **receiver confirmations only** (NOOR rule — giver reports shown struck-through: visible for debugging, inadmissible for decisions); **(5)** the registrar feed (§7.1) read at the top of every cycle — a new entry is a standing agenda item whether or not the engine acts; **(6)** consult answers since last cycle, with provenance, treated as **suggestions, never evidence**. The picture is appended each cycle to `situation-picture.jsonl` — Tier L's primary in-round evidence, and the reason a post-round reader can reconstruct what the engine could have known. A decision the picture cannot explain is a defect, filed as such.

**The cycle.** Clock **20 s**, staggered: Tier D ticks at **t ≡ 7 (mod 20 s)** of round time, census mini-reads at t ≡ 17 (never convoyed with the cameraman-pattern hold or the perception rotation). Each cycle: assemble picture → run the filed decision rules (deterministic order: abort conditions, then play-completion checks, then the escalation queue) → **at most one model call** (`syndicate-captain`, ≤120 output tokens, `enable_thinking:false`) to select among rule-admissible plays or resolve a named ambiguity. **Predictable-default rule:** if the model call times out or the budget denies it, the rules' named default fires (hold current plays; no new activation) — an unthinking Tier D yields a predictable syndicate, never a frozen one; Tier R continues regardless (a late decision is a missed optimisation, never a standing-still citizen).

**The escalation split.** Play-shaped escalations (the book has a play; sequencing two hats; re-planning around a marked obstacle) resolve **at Tier D**, from the filed book, inside the cycle — the overwhelming majority by design. Book-shaped escalations (no play covers it; plays conflict; an unforeseen trigger) are **never improvised**: Tier D fires the book's named default (hold-and-mark), files a `book-gap` in the firing log with the picture line that exposed it, and consults only if the escalation table (§6.3) says the row is consult-shaped.

**Consult budget (spec §2.4).** **4 consults per round, spent only through Tier D**, filed **alternating books** (GLM, MiniMax, GLM, MiniMax). The substrate ceiling binds underneath: the consult books (`commons/consults/glm.jsonl`, `minimax.jsonl`) cap **2 answered consults per citizen per rolling hour per book**, and Tier D files under the captain body's identity — so 4 is exactly the two books' combined ceiling for a ~40-minute round, with nothing to spare. A consult filed is not a consult answered (drivers poll ~60 s; silence is a legal answer; an unanswered consult at the horn is a **filed result, not a fault**). Every answered consult carries its provenance `{seat, model, latencyMs}`; answers without it are inadmissible to Tier L.

## 5. The round, end to end (spec §7's shape, fallback stores)

Two rounds, one day of pilot time. All durations are the spec's pre-registered PROPOSAL values, kept identical to the district form so the two runs stay comparable.

### 5.0 Phase 0 — substrate readiness (before any scripting)

Governor identities written and probe-verified (§2.2); fallback server up on the flat board with the paint layer loaded; board acceptance passed (§1.5); cast joined in order, `.dat`s verified and hashed (§2.1); arena survey filed (§1.4); cameraman-pattern hold configured **for the fallback world** (a fresh instance — the league's guarded cameraman watches the live world; the pilot's hold is its own guarded process on the same discipline: 300 s refresh, each refresh landing-verified in the server log within 8 s, two misses = HOLD-LOST); the GM's registrar file created empty (§7.1); the sealed-change hash filed in the round manifest (§7.2 — **no hash, no drop**). **Gate:** the §8.4 checklist passes in full, assembled by the manager seat and verified by Muse.

### 5.1 Phase 1 — script phase (60 minutes, off-clock)

Tier L drafts **playbook v1** (§6) — re-deriving every timing and coordinate from the arena survey; any line it cannot ground in the survey is cut. In parallel, per Addendum 3's many-directions rule: one seat runs the **trigger-surface audit** (every trigger against §6.1 legality; violations listed, never fixed silently), one seat runs a **red-team read** (where does the book silently assume? which abort has no owner?), and the manager seat assembles v1 with both results appended as the playbook's own process note. Filed: the playbook v1, `adjustment-level.json` v1 (cycle 20 s; re-plan aggressiveness 1; consult table per §4), hat assignments (Round 1 per §2.1), and the pre-registered criteria (§8.1) in the round manifest — all before the drop. Filed versions are immutable; the drop runs the filed bytes.

### 5.2 Phase 2 — drop and play (40 minutes, one held daylight period)

The GM publishes the round in world chat (objective, horn). The cast spawns at the base anchor with empty inventories (scenario kit: splat guns only, uncharged). Play runs per the book. **The round clock is the GM's published horn, not the world's sun.** If HOLD-LOST fires (two unverified refreshes), the round **suspends**: citizens hold position under SO-4, the clock pauses, and the round resumes only on a re-verified hold — or is abandoned and re-run. A play phase never runs on an unverified hold. Drop protocol includes the standing same-hour spawn-country sweep (on flat ground: a sweep of the fenced area for remnant hostiles, filed in the manifest).

### 5.3 The death circuit-breaker (spec §7.2 Phase 2, paint-adapted)

Deaths are counted from server-log records on a 15 s poll — by mechanism, in the gate, not by an operator watching. **3 deaths by one citizen in a round benches that citizen** (it stays at base; the syndicate plays short-handed). **3 deaths across the syndicate in the first 10 minutes abort the round** for a substrate review. "Deaths" are never a citizen statistic beyond this breaker and criterion 2's count — the game's framing has no other use for them (Addendum 2's law: tag-outs instead of deaths; lethal framing forbidden in mechanics, scoring and narration).

### 5.4 Phase 3 — census and scoring (15 minutes)

The horn sounds (GM publishes). Bodies return to base or hold where they stand. The census runs over the Door: per-sector and total marigold coverage, from world state. The manager seat assembles the round record (census + criteria verdicts + failure classification per §8.2 + the comm-cost table per §8.3, with its **independent recount** — §8.3); Muse verifies. No self-report scores anything.

### 5.5 Phase 4 — reflection (45 minutes, off-clock)

Tier L runs per §6.4: reads the evidence pack, files the round's triples, writes playbook v2 (or records a no-change verdict with reasons — a legal outcome), tunes `adjustment-level.json`, sets Round 2's hats. **Then, and only then, the programme seeds the §7.2 change** — the ordering is the calibration: detection must measure Tier L's registrar reading at Round 2's script phase, not its memory of a hint.

### 5.6 Phase 5 — Round 2 (script 45 min → play 40 min → census → reflection)

Round 2's script phase is where §7.3 is decided: the pre-drop register read is an explicit script-phase checklist item, so an UNDETECTED verdict cannot be excused as "nobody told us to look" — the register is a filed input, and missing it is the measured failure. Play, census and reflection repeat Round 1's shape. Round 2's reflection additionally files the Tier-W detection triple and answers, in its own words, *which tier was the Door-move a problem for?* — graded against §7.3 by the manager seat (never by Tier L itself).

## 6. The playbook (spec §3, bound to the fallback board)

Filed artefact at execution: `playbooks/pilot-a-flat/v1.md` on the fallback's Thor-side syndicate scratch (v2 after Round 1's reflection; versions never edited in place). Authorship: Tier L drafts, the seat crew red-teams the trigger surface, the manager seat assembles. **Pilot limits: at most 12 active plays; escalation budget 4 consults; every trigger citizen-local.** Standing orders SO-1…SO-5 (spec §1.3) are incorporated into the playbook header by reference.

### 6.1 The trigger-surface legality rule (binding on every line)

A play may trigger only on state the triggering citizen can itself sense: own position, own inventory, own hp/food, own perception-rotation products, team chat calls actually received, the round clock as published by the game master. A trigger needing global knowledge (another citizen's inventory, the census total, the opponent's state) is **illegal** and the playbook review rejects it — it would fire on a guess or never fire. Tier D's triggers are the exception that proves the rule: Tier D triggers on the situation picture, which is Tier D's local state by construction.

### 6.2 The plays (spec §3.3, coordinates re-derived from the fallback survey at Phase 1)

**PLAY first-light-paint** (hats: all) — the opening play. TRIGGER: round start; citizens at base anchor; GM has published the round. (1) SCOUT runs survey sweep **S1** (base → Door, marked route) and calls `S1-clear` or `S1-hot:<bearing>`; S1-hot twice → FALLBACK **dusk-post** (all hats hold base; Tier D informed by the calls themselves). (2) RUNNER, on `S1-clear`, runs supply leg 1: charge at the base pigment station, carry full charges to **C-DOOR**, deposit, call `C-loaded:<count>` — scores nothing until confirmed (NOOR). (3) BUILDER moves to the Door on the marked route once `S1-clear` sounds; attends C-DOOR only after `C-loaded`; withdraws charges; calls `C-confirmed:<count>` on inventory match; begins **sector A**; calls `sector-A:<done|blocked:<reason>>` at sector end or on block. (4) CAPTAIN (body) holds the base anchor, relays calls; on any hat hp < 10 calls `regroup:base` (all hats comply immediately). **ABORT:** if `C-confirmed` has not sounded by round tick **6000** (≈5 min), Tier D is informed by absence; the play closes as **ABORTED-supply** and the standing default **DOOR-DIRECT** (builder carries own charges from base) activates. COMMS: the quoted strings only; everything else is escalation.

**PLAY noor-handoff** (RUNNER gives, BUILDER receives; applies to C-DOOR and C-BASE) — PRECONDITION: chest named in the play header with published coordinates; manifest M (item, count) listed. (1) RUNNER places M in the chest; calls `<chest>-loaded:<M-hash>`. (2) RECEIVER attends only after the loaded call; withdraws exactly M; calls `<chest>-confirmed:<count>` on inventory match. (3) No confirmation within **600 ticks** → RUNNER re-attends and reports actual contents (`<chest>-actual:<contents>`); Tier D decides. RULE: a transfer counts — for scoring **and** for Tier D's supply picture — only on the receiver's confirmation. The giver's call scores nothing.

**PLAY door-push** (BUILDER paints, RUNNER supplies, SCOUT watches, CAPTAIN holds) — the objective play; fires repeatedly. TRIGGER (BUILDER-local): charges ≥ 1 AND current sector incomplete AND no `regroup` outstanding. (1) BUILDER works the current sector in the filed order **A→B→C→D**; re-scans own sector coverage after each charge spent (census client, local read); on sector complete calls `sector-<X>:done` and advances. (2) RUNNER keeps C-DOOR non-empty — on BUILDER's `charges-low` call (BUILDER-local trigger: own charges ≤ 1) runs the supply loop (NOOR). (3) SCOUT holds the overwatch mark from the survey, rotation products on; on a colour-bearing or obstacle product on the Door approach, calls `approach:<clear|hot:<bearing>>`. (4) Any hat charge-exhausted beyond the loop's ability to refill → SO-3 walk-back; the play continues short-handed. ABORT: the horn, or `regroup:base`.

**PLAY regroup-base** (hats: all) — the fallback play. TRIGGER: the captain's `regroup:base` call received, OR own hp < 10, OR own position off surveyed ground (citizen-local check). (1) All hats stop the current step and walk the marked route to the base anchor. (2) On arrival call `in:<hat>`; the CAPTAIN (body) counts arrivals from calls received (captain-local: calls are state the captain senses). (3) The play closes when Tier D issues the next activation or the horn sounds.

|---|---|---|
| Route blocked / S1-hot twice | Scout | Fallback dusk-post; Tier D decides re-survey vs reroute (play-shaped) |
| Supply unconfirmed > 600 ticks | Runner | NOOR step 3; Tier D (play-shaped) |

### 6.4 Tier L — reflection between rounds (spec §5)

## 7. Tier W — referee, registrar, and the sealed change

### 7.1 The game master as host + registrar (spec §6.1)

The league's game master pattern (Thor `~/game-master/` — staged, receipted, landing-verified world-parameter nudges; changes world settings only, never a citizen's code) wears the Pilot A host hat for the fallback, on the fallback's scratch stores. **Round host functions (mechanical):** publish the round (objective, horn time, conditions) in world chat at drop; run the landing-verified daylight hold in harness (§5.0); publish the horn; snapshot the final census request. It stages and receipts; **it does not score** — scoring is read from the census over world state, a store the GM does not write. **Change registrar functions:** every world-change the GM makes or is directed to make during the pilot — weather, the seeded change of §7.2, any substrate fix — is appended to the **register file** (fallback scratch path, filed at Phase 0: `registrar.jsonl`, entries `{ts, round, kind, description, receipt-ref}`, with adoption 1's two added keys on `incident`/`nudge` entries: `fault_owner` + `fault_owner_evidence`; `board-change` entries stay clean — a registered change is a fact, not a fault) in addition to its human receipt log (whose nudge/incident entries likewise carry the `**Fault owner:**` line, adoption 1). The register is the Tier-W event log: Tier D reads it every cycle, Tier L reads it in reflection — and **a change not in the register does not count as announced**. The GM registers and timestamps; it never interprets a change for the syndicate. Between rounds the GM restores the arena's scenario parameters to the playbook-filed baseline, and the register records the restoration too.

### 7.2 The seeded change, hash-sealed (spec §6.2 + adoption 2's ceremony, full)

**The change: THE DOOR MOVES.** Between Round 1's reflection and Round 2's drop, the Vault Door monument is demolished at site A and rebuilt — same dimensions (9 × 12), same sector grid — at **site B, roughly double the supply-leg distance (200 → 400 blocks)**, on the fallback's flat ground (delta D4: no ridge; the scout must still re-survey the route and marks). Why this change (spec, restated): every Round 1 play is silently addressed to the old site — routes, the S1 sweep, C-DOOR's published coordinates, sector approach marks. A syndicate that merely *patches* (updates a coordinate) keeps a book whose timings, abort ticks (6000), NOOR windows (600 ticks) and supply economics were all sized for half the distance, and they go quietly wrong. The correct response is a **re-period**: new survey, re-sized windows, possibly a forward cache chest — a different playbook, not an edited coordinate. The change is **registered but not announced**: it appears in `registrar.jsonl` (kind `board-change`) and the GM's receipt log, and nowhere in world chat. Discovering it is the syndicate's — Tier D's feed read, the scout's S1 sweep finding an empty pad, or Tier L's pre-drop register read.

**The seal ceremony (adoption 2, five steps, roles named):** writer and holder are different seats; Tier L and its operators are neither.

1. **Write** (before Round 1's script phase ends): the writer — the manager seat — writes the change's full specification to `sealed-tierw-round2.txt`, containing exactly: the change kind; site A's coordinates; site B's coordinates; the monument's dimensions and sector grid; the supply-distance consequence in blocks; the exact text that will appear in `registrar.jsonl` at seeding time; the restoration note. Nothing about detection grades or expected playbook responses goes in the file — it specifies the *world*, not the hoped-for verdict.
2. **Hash and file** (before Round 1 begins): the writer computes the file's sha256 and files in the **round manifest**: the hex digest, the filename, the writer's seat identity, the UTC timestamp. **No hash in the manifest, no Round 1 drop** (checklist item, §8.4). The plaintext does not enter the manifest, the repo, the playbook directory, or Tier L's reach.
3. **Hold:** the holder — Muse (the parent lane), not the manager seat — keeps the sole plaintext copy **off the Thor** (Oracle-side, mode `0600`, outside any git working tree). Tier L runs on Thor-side evidence packs; the plaintext is in none of them, and the spec's §6.2 is **excluded from Tier L's script-phase input list** for Round 2 — the exclusion named in the manifest's input list, so an UNDETECTED verdict can be neither excused by nor credited against a document Tier L was never given. Pre-drop verification (the adoption's cheapest check): the manifest holds a 64-hex digest + writer + timestamp, and the holder returns a one-line receipt — its own `sha256sum` of the held file — matching the manifest digest digit-for-digit; no plaintext crosses.
4. **Seed** (after Round 1's reflection closes — §5.5's ordering): the GM executes the change by retrieving the plaintext from the holder at seeding time. The register entry it writes is deliberately terse: `{ts, round: 2, kind: "board-change", description: "sealed board-change <first 8 hex of the filed digest>", receipt-ref: <GM receipt id>}` — announcing *that* a change exists without leaking *what* it is beyond what a scout's sweep would find anyway.
5. **Reveal** (at Round 2 scoring, after the census, before the manager seat classifies the playbook diff): the holder publishes the plaintext into the round record; anyone can re-run `sha256sum` against the manifest digest filed before Round 1. A mismatch **voids the §7.3 calibration** (filed as a substrate failure, `fault_owner: harness` — the registrar process failed, not the citizens) and the change is re-sealed and re-seeded for a re-run round.

**Honest limit (adoption 2, stated in the open):** for Pilot A the change's *identity* (the Door moves) is known to the programme's designers — it is printed here and in the spec. The seal protects what a verdict could be retrofitted on: the exact new site, the exact distance, the seeding timestamp relative to reflection closing, and the register text.

### 7.3 The detection calibration (spec §6.3, pre-registered, three grades)

Scored from filed artefacts only:

- **DETECTED-AND-RE-PERIODED (full pass):** before Round 2's drop, Tier L files the new playbook version in which the Door-move is answered structurally — re-surveyed routes, re-sized NOOR/abort windows, supply plan re-costed — **and** the reflection note cites the registrar entry by timestamp. The version diff is classified by the manager seat (not Tier L) as **swap-class**: ≥ half the active plays materially changed.
- **DETECTED-AND-PATCHED (partial):** the move is noticed and answered, but the diff is patch-class (coordinates changed, windows and economics untouched) — the syndicate saw the fact and missed its size: the exact failure Tier W exists to name.
- **UNDETECTED (fail):** Round 2's book still addresses the old Door and the discovery happens in-round (the scout finds the empty pad). The round still runs — the discovery and recovery are themselves evidence — but the calibration verdict is fail, and Tier L's post-Round-2 reflection must say so in those words.

The calibration judges the **reflection tier's reading discipline, not the citizens**: an UNDETECTED verdict is a Tier L / registrar-feed failure, filed in the script column of §8.2's separation, with its `fault_owner` per adoption 1's reconciliation.

## 8. Criteria, kill conditions, manifests, gates

### 8.1 The five criteria (spec §7.7, pre-registered; failure columns + adoption 1 owners)

| # | Criterion (as run) | Evidence store | Failure column |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Coverage ≥ 60%** of the Door face in syndicate colour at the horn, census-measured (the census *is* the receiver) | Paint census output over server world state | Script if supply/play economics fell short on a healthy substrate; substrate if splats/census misbehaved |
| 2 | **Zero deaths**; zero grind events; **no citizen pinned** (forced-move storm > 60 s at one cell) on surveyed routes | Server log; harness movement logs | A pin on a surveyed route is substrate by definition; a death to a *marked* hazard is script |
| 3 | **≥ 2 plays fire as written** (trigger → sequence → confirmation), no operator intervention | Firing log vs the filed playbook version | Script — unless the firing engine itself failed (substrate) |
| 4 | **Every escalation inside budget** (≤4 consults, alternating books); every answered consult carrying provenance | Consult books + governor ledger | Script if the book over-escalated; substrate if answers were unprovenanced or drivers silent past their poll discipline |

**Kill conditions (spec, restated).** A criterion failed on **substrate** grounds kills the substrate — fix and re-run **once**. A criterion failed on **script** grounds with a healthy substrate (plays don't fire, consults exhaust by mid-round, coverage < 25%) kills §6's budget values and the playbook's design assumptions — they are redesigned before any Pilot B conversation. Round 2's §7.3 verdict is reported **alongside, never averaged into**, these five.

*Flat-board reading of the criteria, filed so the fallback's results are never over-read:* criterion 2's pin clause and criterion 5's envelope are near-vacuous on flat ground served at settled rates — passing them here proves the *mechanisms* (pin detection, cadence accounting) work, not that they would pass under district relief or contention. ## 10. Non-goals — what this fallback does not establish

- **Carry-over, bounded:** playbook versions, Tier D rule files and Tier L's triples from a fallback run are legitimate *drafts and library entries* for the district run — but the district run's Phase 1 re-derives its playbook from the district survey (spec §7.2), and no fallback artefact enters it by right.
